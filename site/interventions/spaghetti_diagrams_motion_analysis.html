Spaghetti Diagrams / Motion Analysis

This module teaches a simple observation and diagramming method: watch a person, a material, or a tool move through a real process, trace its actual path onto a floor plan, and use what the tangled result shows to shorten it. It ends with at least one trialled layout or method change, a redrawn "after" path, and that improved path locked into the standard so it doesn't quietly drift back.

Effort

What it takes

Time
A representative observation period, typically one full cycle or a fixed block of time long enough to capture normal variation, not a single idealised pass. Drawing the diagram itself takes minutes once observation is done.
Who's involved
Whoever will observe and trace the path, and, wherever practical, the person actually doing the walking or moving, since their own reaction to seeing the diagram is often what turns a data point into a genuine trial.
Materials
A simple floor plan or sketch of the area, accurate enough to place fixed points correctly. A pen, or a simple digital equivalent, is enough; no specialist software or tracking equipment is required.

Common questions

FAQ

Do we need special software or tracking equipment to do this?

No. A simple floor plan and a pen are enough for the standard method. Digital tracking exists as a newer alternative, but the traditional pen-on-paper approach is deliberately the default here, and it's usually all a site needs. See Pro and Essentials section 4.

Should we trace the person, the material, or both?

Start with whichever the suspected problem actually points to: a person's path if the concern is walking distance or repeated reaching, a material's path if the concern is transport distance or a confusing route between process steps. If both genuinely need tracing, draw them as two separate diagrams over the same period rather than combining them into one. See Pro and Essentials section 5 and Pro section 18's first fork.

How is this different from the 8 Wastes module?

8 Wastes gives the site a shared vocabulary for spotting all eight wastes, including motion and transportation, at a broad level. This module is the deeper observation method for those two specifically, used once motion or transport has already been flagged as a genuine priority. See Pro and Essentials section 10.

What if drawing the real path feels awkward or intrusive to the person being observed?

That reaction is common and worth addressing directly rather than pushing past it. The honest framing: the diagram is about the path, not an evaluation of the person walking it, and the goal is shortening the walk, never judging how well anyone does their job. See Pro section 17's first awkward conversation.

We're a highly automated line with limited people movement. Does this module still apply?

Yes, using the material-flow variant: trace parts, components or work in progress between fixed points instead of human movement, since that's usually where the real waste has shifted to on an automated line. See Pro and Essentials section 11 and Pro section 14's first variant.
