Action & Countermeasure Tracking System

What gets agreed in the room, actually happens — every open action visible, owned, dated and genuinely verified, in one place.

Overview

Every functioning daily management system generates actions — a tier meeting surfaces one, a Gemba walk finds another, a layered process audit flags a third — and each of those moments works exactly as intended right up until the meeting ends, at which point the action goes somewhere nobody can see it again. Building tracking separately into each source routine doesn't fix this; it produces exactly the failure this module exists to prevent — multiple competing lists, one per source, none of them trusted.

This module is deliberately thin: a single register with six fields — action, source, owner, due date, status, and a verification note — and nothing more. It doesn't do the problem-solving itself, and it doesn't replace A3, RCA or Kaizen; it only tracks what those routines produce through to a genuinely checked closure. The distinction that makes it work is verification: closure requires a specific note about what was actually checked, by someone other than the owner, not just the word "done" typed against a status field.

Done well, overdue items are visible without hunting for them and get discussed rather than quietly re-dated, and the register sits inside a meeting that's already happening rather than competing for its own slot on the calendar.

At a glance
Time

Setting up the register itself takes under an hour. The real cost is loading the currently open actions from the last couple of weeks — typically half a day the first time, since it usually means pulling scattered actions off flip charts, audit forms and memory.

Who's involved

Whoever owns the register day to day, and everyone who currently generates actions through a tier meeting, Gemba walk, audit, or problem-solving routine. Verification always sits with someone other than the action's owner — self-verification is named as the single most common way this module quietly stops working.

For your own site, decide: who owns the register day to day, updating status and chasing overdue items; who has the authority to verify a closure; where the register lives, and whether that's the same place the tier meeting or board review already happens; and what happens when an action crosses a boundary between shifts, areas, or functions and ownership becomes unclear.

What you'll need

The working file, P2.7_Action_Register.xlsx — one tab, deliberately, holding only the six fields (action, source, owner, due date, status, verification note) and nothing else. It calculates days overdue and a verdict per row, and across all rows how many are open, overdue, and what proportion of closures were genuinely verified. Access to whatever the last couple of weeks of source routines (tier meetings, Gemba walks, audits, problem-solving sessions) have already generated, to load in at setup.

Before you start

This depends on at least one upstream generator — tier meetings, Gemba, layered process audit, or a problem-solving routine — already running, so there's a genuine flow of actions to track rather than an empty register. Agree upfront, explicitly, that "done" means verified, not promised. Avoid the single most common setup failure: creating this register alongside existing lists rather than instead of them.

If more than one place already captures actions (a whiteboard, a meeting note, an audit form), this module replaces those rather than adding a seventh — consolidate into the single register and retire the old lists explicitly.
