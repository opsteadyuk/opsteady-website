NPI / Launch Readiness Pack

This module gives a site a structured way to check, before a new product, process or significant change goes live, that everything it depends on is actually ready: standards written and taught, people trained and signed off, quality controls proven, capacity and rate credible, materials available, and care routines in place for any new equipment. It ends in a genuine go or no-go decision, made on evidence rather than on a date fixed months earlier for reasons that may no longer apply.

What it takes

Time
A short setup to agree categories and build the view, then a fixed rhythm of brief readiness reviews from the point the launch is confirmed until the process is running stably. 10 to 15 minutes is usually enough per review once the view exists.

Common questions

FAQ

Is this the same as a formal APQP or corporate stage-gate process?

No, and it isn't meant to be. It's a practical, plant-level readiness checklist and review rhythm, built from modules the site may already have. Where a formal APQP, PPAP or corporate stage-gate process already exists, Pro covers mapping this view onto those existing gates rather than running two systems side by side. See Pro section 11 and section 13.

Can we launch if a category still shows a gap?

Only if the gap has been explicitly, visibly accepted by whoever owns the overall go or no-go decision, not because nobody got round to closing it. A gap that's simply still open when the date arrives is exactly the pattern this module exists to prevent. See Pro and Essentials section 8.

What are the standard readiness categories?

Standards, skills, quality controls, capacity and rate, material readiness, and asset care. Not every launch touches all six; drop or add categories to match what this specific launch actually requires. See Pro and Essentials section 5.

Who should own the overall readiness decision?

Usually whoever is accountable for the launch itself, a plant manager or a nominated project lead. It needs to be one clear person, agreed in advance, so the call isn't negotiated fresh under pressure in the final week. See Pro and Essentials section 4.

How is this different from a general project plan or Gantt chart?

A project plan tracks tasks and dates. This module tracks readiness, whether the things a launch depends on are genuinely proven, not just scheduled. A task can be marked complete on a plan while the underlying capability still isn't actually proven; the readiness view exists specifically to catch that gap. See Pro section 2's mechanism.
