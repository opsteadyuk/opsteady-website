Common questions

FAQ

How does this connect to Downtime Tracking?

Directly. Downtime Tracking's Pareto is where the critical asset list comes from, assets whose breakdowns are showing up as preventable rather than genuinely unplanned. This module builds a schedule around those specific assets and is reviewed monthly against that same Pareto, so the two stay connected instead of drifting into separate numbers nobody reconciles. See guide section 10.

Do we need a formal PM schedule before Downtime Tracking's planned/unplanned split can work?

No. That was an open question at the framing stage and it's been resolved: Downtime Tracking's standard events register stands on its own and doesn't wait on this module. This module is a later upgrade to how planned maintenance standards get built and protected, not a precondition for honest logging to start. See the guide's opening note.

What if production keeps pushing PM windows back?

That's the single most common way this dies. Decide in advance who has the authority to protect a scheduled PM window, before the first real pressure test arrives, and treat a window that's bumped more than once on the same asset as an escalation, not a routine reschedule. See guide section 9 and section 18 (Pro).

Should we schedule every asset on site straight away?

No. Start with the critical assets Downtime Tracking's Pareto has already flagged. A schedule covering three assets that are genuinely protected beats one covering everything with nothing actually honoured. See guide section 4 and section 5.

What if the PM interval was set by the manufacturer and someone wants to stretch it?

That's not a scheduling decision, it's a compliance one. A manufacturer-mandated interval isn't adjustable by local judgement, however reasonable the pressure feels in the moment. See guide section 5's minimum decision fork and section 14 (Pro).
