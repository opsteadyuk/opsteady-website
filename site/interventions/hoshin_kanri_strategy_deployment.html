Hoshin Kanri / Strategy Deployment

A small number of breakthrough objectives, genuinely negotiated with the people who have to deliver them, visible alongside your daily KPIs and reviewed on a fixed rhythm — not a strategy poster nobody revisits.

£349Buy now
Overview

Every site has a strategy, but most of it lives in a slide deck from the annual planning meeting: presented once, then never mentioned again until next year's version replaces it. Meanwhile the floor runs on its own logic, whatever's most urgent this week. Neither is dishonest exactly, they're just disconnected — built by different people, in different language, with no working link between them. That disconnect happens because strategy usually gets written by people who don't run the floor and handed down as a finished document, with no mechanism for anyone below to say "that's not deliverable as written" before it launches.

Hoshin Kanri closes that gap. It forces a genuinely small number of breakthrough objectives — 3 to 5, not a long list dressed up as priorities — and it runs catchball, a real two-way conversation between the people who set direction and the people who deliver it, so every target is tested against actual capacity before it's fixed. Each breakthrough cascades through one or two annual priorities down to the process-level metrics a supervisor already watches, all recorded on a one-page X-matrix.

Done well, the breakthroughs sit visibly alongside the daily and weekly KPIs, not filed away in a separate strategy folder. Every owner can explain their own contribution in their own words, and when a monthly or quarterly review shows a breakthrough is off track, something actually changes — a resource gets reallocated, a lower priority gets dropped to make room — rather than the review simply noting the colour and moving on.

At a glance
Time

The first pass — from choosing breakthroughs to a signed-off X-matrix — typically takes 2 to 3 weeks of real calendar time. Not because the document itself is slow to fill in, but because catchball needs genuine back-and-forth with the people who'll own the process metrics, and that can't be rushed to fit a single afternoon.

Who's involved

The site leadership team, who agree the breakthroughs, and every process-level owner whose metric ends up linked to one of them — brought in during catchball, not told the outcome afterward.

For your own site, decide: who has final say if catchball surfaces genuine disagreement; who owns each breakthrough at site level, and who owns each process metric underneath it; who sees the full X-matrix versus only their own slice; and who has the authority to reallocate resource when a monthly review shows a breakthrough is off track.

What you'll need

KPI Blueprint Map (F1.1), in at least partial use, so a measure tree already exists to deploy against — this module doesn't build that tree, it decides which parts of it get resourced this year.

The working file is F5.1_X_Matrix_and_Review_Log.xlsx, two tabs: an X-matrix tab recording the four panels (direction, breakthroughs, priorities, process metrics) with named owners and a catchball check, and a breakthrough review log tab, one row per breakthrough per review period.

Before you start

Start with an honest conversation, ideally with the site leadership team together in one room, about what the site is and isn't trying to become over the next 1 to 3 years — that conversation is the raw material the breakthroughs get drafted from, not the X-matrix itself. Draft the breakthroughs from it and keep the list to 3 to 5, resisting the pull to stretch it to 8 so no function feels left out. Run catchball as a genuine sequence: draft breakthroughs down to process owners, listen to what comes back about capacity and conflicting priorities, revise, and only then treat the matrix as fixed for the year. Set the review rhythm — monthly for process-level metrics, quarterly for the breakthroughs themselves — before the matrix is finalised, so the cadence isn't an afterthought bolted on once the document's done.

If catchball is new to your site, it's easy to run it as a one-way announcement with a Q&A bolted on — that isn't catchball, it's a slower version of top-down.
