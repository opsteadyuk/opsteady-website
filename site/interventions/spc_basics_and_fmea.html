SPC Basics & FMEA

This module teaches two connected tools, kept deliberately practical. Statistical process control, SPC, is a way of plotting a critical measurement over time so that ordinary variation and a genuine problem can be told apart, before the problem produces a defect. Failure mode and effects analysis, FMEA, is a structured way of thinking through what could go wrong with a process before it runs, so the highest-risk failures get a countermeasure in advance rather than a reaction afterwards.

TimeSetting a first control chart up, choosing the measure and collecting enough points to set preliminary limits, usually takes one to two weeks of the process running normally. A first-pass process FMEA for a single process typically takes a half-day to a full day of focused time with the right people in the room, not spread thinly across weeks.
Who's involvedFor SPC, whoever already collects or can easily collect the measurement, and whoever owns the reaction when a point goes out of limits. For FMEA, the people who actually run the process, plus anyone with technical or engineering knowledge of how it can fail; a room without the operator misses failure modes only they would know about.
MaterialsA simple chart, on paper or a basic spreadsheet, is enough to start. No specialist SPC software is required for a single critical process. A blank process FMEA template, structured by process step.
