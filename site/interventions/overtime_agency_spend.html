Common questions

FAQ

What if we don't have a labour baseline yet?

Agree one, however rough, before logging anything. Without a defined baseline, "overtime" doesn't mean the same thing in every area, and every number that follows is built on sand. Labour Planning & Shift Pattern Model gives you a proper one if it's built, but an agreed baseline headcount is enough to start. See guide section 4 and section 5, fork 1.

Isn't this just extra tracking on top of what finance already does?

Finance already tracks the total. This tracks the cause. The two answer different questions, finance's total tells you spend is up, this tells you why and which pillar can actually fix it. See guide section 2 and section 17.

What if most of our overtime doesn't fit neatly into one category?

Log it against whichever cause is dominant for that instance, and note the secondary factor if it matters. The categories exist to route the fix, not to capture every nuance of a messy real-world shift. If "other" starts dominating because too much doesn't fit, that's the signal to revisit the category list itself. See guide section 9 and section 16, failure 1.

How is this different from just watching the overtime budget line?

A budget line tells you the number went up. It never tells you why, so the response is always the same blunt instruction, bring it down, which rarely changes anything because the underlying cause was never touched. See guide section 13.

What happens once a cause gets routed to another module?

It needs a real owner and a follow-up status, tracked until the receiving pillar's actually picked it up. A routed cause that's logged and then never followed up is one of the clearest ways this module stops earning its keep. See guide section 6 and section 16, failure 5.
