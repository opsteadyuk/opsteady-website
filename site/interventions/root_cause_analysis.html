Common questions

FAQ

How is this different from A3 Problem-Solving Templates (P7.4)?

A3 already verifies root cause before a countermeasure is written, that's part of its own structure. This toolkit supplies the methods, 5 Whys and fishbone, that feed into that step. It's the "how do we find the cause" tool, A3 is the "how do we structure and close the whole problem" tool. This toolkit's verified cause becomes an input to A3's root-cause section, not a competing investigation. See guide section 10.

What if the room already agrees on "operator error"?

That's precisely the moment to slow down. Agreement in a room isn't evidence, however confident everyone sounds. Verify the proposed cause against records, repeat instances or direct observation before accepting it, even if it turns out the room was right all along. See guide section 2 and section 17.

How do we know whether to use 5 Whys or a fishbone?

If the problem looks like a single, traceable thread, use 5 Whys. If it keeps branching into multiple plausible factors rather than converging on one answer, switch to a fishbone and map the candidates properly. See guide section 5, fork 1, and section 18, fork 3.

How many "whys" is enough?

As many as it takes to stop describing a symptom and start describing something that can actually be fixed. "Missed the check" is a symptom. "The changeover standard was never updated" is something you can act on. It's rarely exactly 5, that's a name, not a rule. See guide section 13.

Do we need a fresh problem to practise on, or can we use something already closed?

Use a real, current, trended problem, ideally the top item from Non-Conformance Tracker or Customer Complaint. It doesn't need to be the worst one, just real. Revisiting an already-closed deviation is also a legitimate first exercise if the original conclusion feels shaky. See guide section 4 and section 7.
