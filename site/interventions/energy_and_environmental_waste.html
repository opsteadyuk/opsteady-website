Energy & Environmental Waste

This module extends the same waste-and-cost lens the site already applies to scrap, downtime and overtime to energy, water, materials and environmental impact: compressed air leaks, idle equipment running when it doesn't need to, excessive heating or cooling, water use, packaging or material waste. It builds a simple, ranked view of the main loss sources, rough cost or impact estimates, named owners, and at least one live improvement already underway.

Common questions

FAQ

Is this a carbon-accounting or full environmental management system?

No. It's a practical operational loss-and-cost view, not an ISO 50001 implementation or a carbon-accounting package. Where a formal system already exists, Pro covers connecting it to the same daily operational loss view rather than running two separate systems. See Pro and Essentials section 2 and Pro section 11.

We don't have precise energy or environmental data. Can we still start?

Yes, and you should. Rough estimates, using utility bills, typical loss rates for known issues like compressed air leaks, and simple observation, are enough to rank and start acting on the biggest opportunities. Waiting for perfect data before starting is itself one of this module's named failure modes. See Pro and Essentials section 5 and Pro section 16's second failure.

Isn't environmental performance HSE's responsibility?

The boundary is explicit. HSE owns compliance, permits and the statutory register. This module owns the everyday operational question, what's being wasted and what it costs, the same way a compressed air leak is an operational fix even though pressurised-system safety is an HSE concern. See Pro and Essentials section 10 and Pro section 17's fourth awkward conversation.

How do we decide whether a loss is big enough to escalate into Cost Deployment?

Ask whether it would plausibly sit inside the site's top 10 known operational losses if ranked honestly against everything else. If the answer is close, escalate it anyway. Over-including a loss source costs far less than leaving a genuinely material one sitting invisibly outside the main prioritisation view. See Pro section 15's judgement calls.

Should we look at energy first, or material and environmental waste first?

Start with whichever the suspected problem actually points to: energy-related sources are usually found walking areas during stopped or slow production, looking and listening for what's still running; material and environmental waste is usually found by looking at what leaves the site as waste or excess material. If both seem significant, cover both in the same first pass rather than treating them as separate exercises. See Pro and Essentials section 5's minimum decision fork.
