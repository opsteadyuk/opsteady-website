Heijunka & Takt Time

This module teaches two connected ideas that sit between capacity planning and the daily schedule. Takt time is the pace, calculated from real customer demand and the time genuinely available to produce it, at which the site needs to complete one unit to keep up with what customers actually want, no faster and no slower. Heijunka is the practice of levelling volume, and where needed mix, over a chosen horizon, so the site runs to that steady pace instead of chasing whatever order feels most urgent that hour.

What it takes

Time
Calculating takt and building a first levelled plan for one product family typically takes a few days once real demand data is in hand. Trialling the levelled sequence and adjusting it usually needs one to two horizons, weeks or pitches depending on what was chosen, before it's ready to lock as the standard.
Who's involved
Whoever holds real demand data, usually planning or a customer-facing function, since a takt calculated from an estimate rather than genuine order history will mislead everyone who relies on it. Whoever schedules the floor day to day, since they will be the one actually running the levelled sequence once it's built.
Materials
A simple takt calculation sheet and a levelled plan, on paper or a basic spreadsheet. A heijunka device, physical or a simple visual board, does not require specialist equipment; a slotted card holder or a marked sequence board is standard and inexpensive.

Common questions

FAQ

Is takt time the same as cycle time?

No, and confusing the two is one of the most common mistakes with this module. Cycle time is how long a process actually takes to produce one unit. Takt time is how fast the process needs to run to match real customer demand. The two can match, but they answer different questions, and a process can have a perfectly fine cycle time that's still badly out of step with takt if demand has shifted. See Pro section 5.

If we can run faster than takt, isn't that a good thing?

No, and this is the single biggest mindset shift this module asks for. Running ahead of takt just builds inventory the customer hasn't ordered yet, which sits as cost and risk rather than genuine progress. The goal is a steady pace that matches real demand, not the fastest pace the process can manage. See Pro section 16's first failure and section 17.

Do we need finite-scheduling software to do this?

No. A takt calculation sheet and a simple physical or basic-digital heijunka board are enough to start. This module is deliberately scoped below a full advanced planning and scheduling system. See Pro section 2.

What if our demand genuinely doesn't behave predictably enough for a fixed takt?

That's a real possibility worth testing honestly against actual data first, since some demand that looks unpredictable turns out to have a pattern nobody had levelled yet. For demand that's genuinely too variable, Pro covers three alternatives: a shorter repeating pitch, a frozen near-term horizon, or levelling volume only while letting mix respond within that steady pace. See Pro section 11 and section 18's first fork.

How often do we need to recalculate takt?

Whenever demand or available capacity changes materially, a new contract, a seasonal shift, an equipment or headcount change, not on a fixed calendar schedule regardless of whether anything's actually moved. A takt left stale after a real change quietly stops matching reality. See Pro section 8 and section 16's second failure.
