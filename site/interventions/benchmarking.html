Benchmarking

This module teaches a practical, honest way to compare the site's performance and practices against internal best, sister sites, or external references, on a small number of critical metrics, with real, aligned definitions, and a genuine action attached to the biggest gaps. It's not a data-collection exercise. It ends with at least one gap that has a named owner and a specific method already linked to closing it.

What it takes

Time
Selecting metrics and aligning definitions typically takes longer than collecting the actual comparison data; budget real time for the definition conversation, since it's the step most often rushed and most likely to invalidate the whole comparison if skipped.
Who's involved
Whoever owns the operational metrics being compared, and, where the comparison is internal or cross-site, a genuine counterpart at the comparison site willing to align definitions honestly rather than just hand over a number.
Materials
Existing KPI definitions for the metrics being compared. Access to at least one credible comparison source, an internal best performer, a sister site, or a trustworthy external reference.

Common questions

FAQ

Is this a full external benchmarking programme or a consulting study?

No. It's a practical way to select a small number of genuinely comparable metrics, make the comparison honest, and turn the biggest gaps into owned action. Where a formal corporate benchmarking programme already exists, Pro covers connecting the plant-level view to it rather than duplicating it. See Pro and Essentials section 2 and Pro section 11.

What matters most: how impressive the gap looks, or how comparable the metrics actually are?

Comparability, always. A dramatic-looking gap built on mismatched definitions is noise, not information, and any action taken on the strength of it is built on a false premise. Confirming both sides are measuring the same thing, over comparable time periods, is the step most worth protecting time for. See Pro and Essentials section 4 and Pro section 16's first failure.

Should we benchmark against an internal source or an external one?

Internal comparisons, a sister site or an internal best performer, are usually the more reliable starting point, since definitions can be aligned directly with a real counterpart through honest conversation. External comparisons, against a published industry figure, deserve proportionately more caution, since there's rarely a direct conversation available to verify exactly what's being measured. See Pro and Essentials section 5's minimum decision fork.

What if we don't have a source willing to share real numbers with us?

An internal comparison, against a sister site, a best-performing shift, or the site's own best historical period, is a legitimate and often more reliable starting point than an external source that won't engage honestly on definitions. See Pro section 17's third awkward conversation.

How many metrics should we benchmark at once?

A genuinely small number, three to five as a starting point. Spreading the comparison across everything the site tracks makes it far harder to keep definitions properly aligned, and the whole exercise tends to collapse under its own weight. See Pro and Essentials section 7 and Pro section 16's fifth failure.
