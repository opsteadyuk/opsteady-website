Autonomous Maintenance Starter Pack

Put basic equipment care back at the point of use. Most sites treat equipment care as maintenance's job, full stop. The operator runs the machine, and if something's wrong, they call it in and wait.

£179Buy now
Overview

The trouble is a maintenance team, however good, can't be standing at every asset all day, every day. The operator already is. A noise that's slightly different, a drift in temperature, a fastener working loose, these are the earliest signs of a problem, and the person best placed to notice them is whoever's already there.

At a glance
Time
Half a day to define checks for the pilot asset and brief the first operator, once the asset and its basic failure modes are understood.
Who's involved
The operator who'll run the pilot, since their buy-in decides whether this becomes routine or drops off. Whoever owns maintenance for that asset, since they need to agree what "normal" actually means and commit to acting on flagged findings quickly. Whoever owns Downtime Tracking's Pareto, since the pilot asset should come from there.
What you'll need
One asset to pilot on, ideally one already flagged as high-value from Downtime Tracking's Pareto. A basic understanding of that asset's common early failure signs, from whoever maintains it.
