Autonomous Maintenance Starter Pack

Most sites treat equipment care as maintenance's job, full stop. The operator runs the machine, and if something's wrong, they call it in and wait. The trouble is a maintenance team, however good, can't be standing at every asset all day, every day. The operator already is. A noise that's slightly different, a drift in temperature, a fastener working loose, these are the earliest signs of a problem, and the person best placed to notice them is whoever's already there.

Common questions

FAQ

How is this different from a full TPM rollout?

This module isn't a full Total Productive Maintenance programme. It's one focused piece of it, Autonomous Maintenance, started small and deliberately: one asset, one operator, a handful of genuinely doable checks. Sites carrying a larger, formal TPM system from elsewhere can use this as a real, adapted starting move rather than compliance theatre imposed wholesale. See the guide's opening note.

Where does the pilot asset come from?

Downtime Tracking's Pareto, ideally one already flagged as high-value. Starting with an asset that's actually driving loss makes the first early catch, if and when it happens, immediately credible rather than abstract. See guide section 4 and section 5.

What happens if a flagged finding turns out to be nothing?

That's normal, and expected sometimes. Be careful about tightening the definition of "abnormal" reactively after a single false alarm, since that risks missing the next genuine early catch. Review the definition deliberately, against a pattern of findings over time, not against one instance. See guide section 17 (Pro).

Why start with one asset instead of rolling out everywhere at once?

Because a broad launch is exactly the pattern that tends to collapse within a month, once the initial energy fades and nobody's checking whether checks are genuinely still happening. A pilot that's proven on one asset earns the right to expand. A mandate imposed everywhere at once, with nothing yet tested, does not. See guide section 9.

What if operators feel this is maintenance's job being pushed onto them?

Frame it honestly: they're catching problems on the equipment they run, before those problems interrupt their own shift, not absorbing someone else's workload. The trade needs to be real and visible, fewer surprise stops on their own asset, not just claimed. See guide section 17 (Pro).
