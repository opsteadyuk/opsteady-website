8 Wastes (TIMWOODS) Identification Toolkit

This module teaches the eight classic wastes of lean, TIMWOODS: Transport, Inventory, Motion, Waiting, Over-production, Over-processing, Defects, and Skills, meaning under-utilised talent. It gives a site a shared, practical vocabulary for seeing where effort, time and money are being lost, and a simple method for spotting real examples of each waste in the site's own processes, not from a textbook.

Common questions

FAQ

Isn't this just Value Stream Mapping under a different name?

No. VSM maps the overall flow of a process end to end. This module doesn't map flow, it gives the site a shared vocabulary and a spotting method for the eight specific wastes, drawn from real observation in one area. The two work well in sequence, vocabulary first from this module, then flow mapping once the language is already shared. See Pro and Essentials section 2 and section 10.

Do we need to calculate the cost of each waste as part of this?

No, and doing so isn't the point of this module. The job here is naming and ranking by real impact, time, cost or risk, as the group can honestly estimate it. Turning a ranked waste into an actual cost figure is the Waste & Cost-of-Quality Calculator's job, the natural next step once something has been named here. See Pro and Essentials section 10.

What if the walk finds nothing for one of the eight wastes?

Record that honestly rather than forcing an example that doesn't genuinely fit. The real test is whether the group actually stood at the point where that waste would most likely appear and looked specifically, not whether it was dismissed after a moment's thought. See Pro section 15.

Why do Over-processing and Skills waste need special attention?

Because they're consistently the hardest two to spot on a casual walk. Motion and Transport are visible at a glance; Over-processing and Skills require understanding why a step exists or what a person is genuinely capable of, and both get skipped or filled in with a token example more often than any of the others. See Pro section 16's second failure.

Our site has tried similar exercises before and they fizzled out. What's different here?

The named owner per top-ranked waste and a scheduled re-walk trigger, set at the same time the first walk finishes, not added later as an afterthought. Most versions of this exercise fail from having no built-in mechanism to keep it alive, not from the walk itself being weak. See Pro section 9's first failure and section 17.
