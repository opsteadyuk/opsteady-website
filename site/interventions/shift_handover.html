Shift Handover System

The moment the day's truth changes hands is the handover, and it's usually the least structured moment in the whole daily rhythm. One shift knows what actually happened. The next shift inherits whatever got mentioned in passing on the way out the door. In a reactive week that gap compounds: the incoming shift acts on yesterday's picture while today is already writing a new one.

Common questions

FAQ

Why exactly five fields? Wouldn't more detail be safer?

More fields sound safer and work worse. The whole design depends on the handover taking under 2 minutes done honestly, since that's what keeps it running under pressure instead of being the first thing skipped. Five is chosen because it covers the ground that actually matters, safety, quality, production status, outstanding actions, anything abnormal, without adding fields that invite skimming. If a genuine gap keeps showing up that none of the five catches, that's worth raising as a change to the definition, agreed once in writing, not solved by quietly adding a sixth field nobody commits to filling properly. See guide section 2 and section 15.

Can we change the five headings to something that fits our site better?

Yes, that's flexible skin, not the fixed spine. What's fixed is that five fields get stated every time, nothing left blank, and the change gets agreed once, in writing, on the Definitions tab, not decided shift by shift by whoever's handing over that day. See guide section 15.

What actually counts as "abnormal"? Isn't that subjective?

It is subjective, deliberately, because the alternative is a fixed list that goes stale the moment something genuinely unusual happens that nobody thought to name in advance. The test is comparative, not definitional: is this different from the normal picture on this line, this shift. If someone's consistently unsure what belongs there, that's worth a direct conversation, not a longer checklist. See guide section 17.

Why does an outstanding action need its own row on the Actions Tracker? Isn't mentioning it in the log enough?

No, and this is the one rule in the whole tool worth being strict about. A mentioned problem is everyone's responsibility, which in practice means nobody's. An owned problem, with a name and a date on the Actions Tracker, either gets done or visibly doesn't, and either way someone can see it. See guide section 2 and section 13.

We're a small site, one shift, same two people every day. Do we still need this?

Probably not the full Pro depth, but the core five-field discipline still holds value even on a stable, small team, memory under pressure is unreliable regardless of team size. If your shift pattern is genuinely simple and stable, Essentials has what you need. See guide section 11.
