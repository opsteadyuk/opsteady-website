Training Plan & Competency Sign-off

A Skills Matrix that's actually being used honestly produces red cells, and that's the matrix doing its job. The problem most sites hit next isn't the matrix, it's what happens after it. A page of red cells is visible, everyone who looks at it can see the gaps, and visibility on its own closes nothing. Weeks pass. The same red cells are still there at the next review, not because nobody cares, but because "someone should sort that" was never attached to a person or a date.

Common questions

FAQ

We don't have a finished Skills Matrix yet. Can we still start a plan?

Yes, and waiting for a finished matrix is usually the wrong call. Build the first plan from whatever the matrix shows today, partial or complete. A plan built from three known single points of failure is worth more right now than a perfect plan built from a matrix that's still weeks from done. See guide section 4.

Training happened, but we forgot to record the sign-off. Is the gap still open?

Yes, until the sign-off lands back on the matrix. A skill that's genuinely been taught and still shows red on the sheet everyone's looking at hasn't closed anything as far as this tool is concerned. The fix isn't a second record, it's treating the sign-off as part of the training happening, not an admin step for later. See guide section 9.

Is this just a second version of what the Skills Matrix already does?

No. The matrix owns the assessment, the ILUO scale, the criteria for each level and the dated sign-off record itself. This module doesn't duplicate any of that. It reads the matrix's gap list and turns each line into a name, a trainer and a real date. Where the two overlap, this plan reads from the matrix, it never writes its own competing version. See the guide's opening distinction and section 10.

How do we tell a single point of failure from a routine gap?

Ask whether only one person can currently do the task unsupervised, or whether other cover already exists. If it's the former, it goes to the top of the list regardless of anything else on the sheet. Everything else is prioritised normally. See guide section 5.

Does the trainer have to be the most senior person on the team?

No, and seniority on its own is the wrong test. The trainer has to already hold Owner on that specific task. The most senior person in the room isn't always the one qualified to sign someone else off on it, and assigning them anyway is one of the ways this tool quietly stops working. See guide section 5.
