Common questions

FAQ

How is this different from Managing Change That Sticks (E2)?

E2 navigates resistance to one specific, named change already underway, working through sceptics, informal leaders, verbal sponsors and the rest. This module is about standing cross-functional participation in improvement work generally, whether or not any particular change is currently in motion. They share some of the same audience, senior sponsors with real influence, but for a genuinely different, ongoing purpose. See guide section 1 and section 17.

How do we know if an initiative genuinely needs another function involved?

Ask three direct questions. Does it touch labour, shift patterns or a contract term? HR has a stake. Does it claim a saving or need capital? Finance has a stake. Does it change sequencing, batch size or capacity in a way that could touch a customer commitment? Sales has a stake. If none of those genuinely apply, proceed without engaging anyone else. See guide section 4 and section 17.

What if we don't have time to loop other departments into every improvement?

You don't need to, only initiatives with a genuine stake, checked honestly first. The stake-check itself takes about an hour, considerably less than unwinding a customer commitment broken by a change nobody checked, or re-pitching a saving the board never credited the first time. See guide section 16.

What's a "specific ask," and why does it matter so much?

A real, answerable question, not a vague invitation to a meeting. "Come and have a look at this" produces a vague, low-value response. "Help me frame this saving the way the board expects to see a payback figure" gives the invited function something concrete to actually answer. A vague ask is the single most common reason this fails. See guide section 15, failure 1.

What if finance or HR pushes back hard on something operations sees as purely operational?

Treat the pushback as the value of having asked, not an obstacle to route around. If their concern holds up, the plan genuinely needs to change. If it doesn't, that's worth a direct conversation about why, not a decision to stop asking next time. See guide section 16.
