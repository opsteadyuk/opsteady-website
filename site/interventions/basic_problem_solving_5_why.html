Basic Problem Solving / 5 Why

This module teaches a lightweight, teachable version of the 5 Why method: describe the problem factually, ask why until you reach a cause that's specific enough to check and fix, propose a countermeasure, and assign an owner and a way to verify it actually held. It's designed to be run in minutes, at the workstation, by the people who found the problem, not filed away for a specialist to look at later.

Common questions

FAQ

Does it always have to be exactly five whys?

No. Five is a guide, not a rule. Stop asking why once the answer is both actionable, something a countermeasure can target, and checkable, something someone could go and verify, whether that takes three questions or seven. See Pro and Essentials section 5.

How is this different from Root Cause Analysis or A3?

This module is the everyday, floor-level version, run in minutes by whoever found the problem. Root Cause Analysis and A3 are the escalation path for problems that turn out to be bigger, cross-functional, or recurring despite a verified fix. This module isn't a smaller version of those methods, it's the tool that handles the great majority of problems that never need them. See Pro and Essentials section 10 and Pro section 18.

What if the "why" chain keeps pointing at a person?

Treat that as a sign the chain stopped too early, not as the answer. "The operator missed a step" is almost always a mid-chain answer; ask why that step was missed, what about the process made it likely, rather than accepting it as final. See Pro section 16's second failure and section 17's first awkward conversation.

Do we need a specialist to run a 5 Why?

No, that's the point. It's designed to be run by any supervisor or team leader, at the workstation, with the people who found the problem. A coaching role reviews completed sheets and helps sharpen the questions, but doesn't need to be present for every one. See Pro and Essentials section 4.

What happens if the countermeasure doesn't actually fix the problem?

That's exactly what the verification step is for. If the problem recurs after a countermeasure was genuinely checked and confirmed working, that's a real escalation signal, worth treating as a candidate for full Root Cause Analysis rather than a second attempt at the same lightweight fix. See Pro section 18's fifth fork.
