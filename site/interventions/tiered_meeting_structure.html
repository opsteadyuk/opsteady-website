Tiered Meeting Structure

Most sites have too many meetings and too little structure behind them. Some problems get raised in five different rooms before anyone actually decides what to do about them. Others never get raised anywhere that can act on them, and sit unresolved at the bottom for months. Both failures look different and cost the same thing: senior time spent on the wrong problems, and real problems left to fester at the level that can least afford to carry them.

Common questions

FAQ

We already have too many meetings and don't know where to start. Do we design the new structure first, or fix the old one?

Map first. List every recurring meeting on the site, who attends and how long each genuinely runs, before designing a single tier. Most sites are surprised how many meetings turn out to be duplicating each other under different names once they're all in one place. See guide section 5.

What happens to the meetings this replaces?

They get cancelled, not left running alongside the new structure. A tier structure that sits on top of the old meetings, rather than actually retiring them, is one of the more common ways this fails. See guide section 5 and section 9.

Does Tier 1 really need to be daily, even on a site that isn't fast-moving?

Yes. Tier 1 is where problems get caught while they're still small and cheap to fix. A team that only reviews its own SQDC board every few days is finding out about Monday's problem on Thursday, which defeats the entire purpose of the board underneath it. See guide section 3.

How many tiers does our site actually need?

Most sites need three: team, area, site. A few genuinely need four, where a real fourth management layer exists and would actually review something distinct. Don't default to four because an org chart happens to have four boxes on it. See guide section 5's minimum decision fork.

What if an item gets escalated but doesn't actually meet the stated criteria?

Send it back down, and say so directly. This is uncomfortable the first few times, but letting criteria bend for anyone who's persistent enough is exactly how the whole structure quietly reverts to everything going to the top. See guide section 9 and section 17.
