Common questions

FAQ

How do we know if something's worth a chartered event versus an A3?

Size and ownership are the test. A smaller, single-owner problem belongs with A3 Problem-Solving Templates. A bigger, cross-functional opportunity, usually Value Stream Mapping's biggest named gap, is worth chartering here. If you're unsure which, it's often a sign the problem hasn't been sized clearly enough yet. See guide section 5, fork 1, and section 10.

What if scope creeps once the team's already assembled?

Point back to the charter's out-of-scope line directly. That line exists precisely for this moment, when a well-intentioned team wants to fix everything now that they're all in a room together. New ideas get logged for a future event, not folded into this one. See guide section 9 and section 17.

Do we need Value Stream Mapping built before we can use this?

In practice, yes, or at least a genuine, sized opportunity identified some other way. Chartering an event around enthusiasm rather than a real trended finding is exactly the pattern behind programmes that fade within weeks. See guide section 4 and section 18, fork 2.

What if we can't free a full team for several consecutive days?

That's covered as a Pro variant, a scaled-down single-day version run against the same charter discipline, problem statement, scope, out-of-scope line, named team, target, measurement plan, just compressed into one protected day. See guide section 11 and section 14.

Why does the charter need a before-and-after-and-later measurement plan, not just before-and-after?

Because a gain measured once on the day the event ends is the easiest number in the whole process to get wrong. Checking again a month later, after the event's energy has worn off, is what actually tells you whether the improvement held or quietly eroded back. See guide section 3 and section 8.
