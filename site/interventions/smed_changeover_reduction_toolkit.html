Common questions

FAQ

What does "internal" and "external" actually mean here?

Internal is any step that genuinely requires the machine to be stopped. External is anything that could happen before or after, machine still running, retrieving tooling, pre-checking, pre-calibrating. Most changeovers have far more genuinely external work bundled into the stopped window than anyone expects, simply because nobody separated the two before. See guide section 4.

Do we need to buy new clamps or fixtures to get started?

No. Most of a first pass's gain comes from converting steps to external and re-sequencing what's left, at zero cost. Equipment like quick-release clamps is a real second-pass tool, not a requirement to see the first result. See guide section 14 and section 16.

How do we pick which changeover to start with?

The one costing the most capacity, by frequency, duration, or both. If Bottleneck Analysis Tool is already in use and the highest-pain changeover sits on the confirmed bottleneck, start there, it's the exact free-first move that tool's own Exploit step calls for. See guide section 17.

What if some steps genuinely can't be converted, a mandatory cleaning cycle or a regulatory hold time?

Name that step explicitly as a real floor the changeover can't shrink past, rather than treating a smaller-than-hoped reduction as a failed pilot. Convert and streamline everything around it. See guide section 13.

Why did the worked example land on 20 minutes instead of the famous "single minute" target?

Because 20 minutes down from 52 is a genuine, honest first-pass result, and the classic SMED target of single-digit minutes typically takes several passes to reach, not one. A site holding a real, written 20-minute changeover has more actual capacity than one still guessing at 52. See guide section 2 and section 13.
