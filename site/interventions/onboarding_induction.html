Onboarding & Induction Pack

Most sites don't have a bad onboarding process. They have no onboarding process, just whoever happens to be free that morning showing a new starter around and hoping the important things get mentioned. Some weeks that's a experienced team leader who covers everything. Some weeks it's whoever's least busy, and "least busy" and "best placed to induct someone" are rarely the same person.

Half a day to build the generic week-one and week-two template, once. From then on, the buddy's time is the real cost: fairly constant through week one, tapering through week two, not a fixed daily slot that ignores how the fortnight actually unfolds.

Whoever owns onboarding on the floor, usually a team leader or supervisor, to build and own the plan. A named buddy for every new starter. Whoever owns the Skills Matrix and Set Standards, so the induction points at what's actually current rather than what someone remembers.

Common questions

FAQ

We're too short-staffed to do this properly right now. Can it wait until things calm down?

That's exactly the moment it matters most, not a reason to hold off. The busiest weeks are when onboarding usually reverts to whoever's free, which is the pattern this tool exists to stop. If a full two-week plan genuinely doesn't fit right now, use the compressed variant, compliance and safety essentials plus first-task sign-off, and say plainly that it's a deliberate shorter version, not a quietly reduced one. See section 14 in the Pro guide.

What if the buddy and the new starter aren't on the same shift?

Sort it before day one. A buddy who's rarely actually on shift with the person they're meant to be supporting isn't functioning as a buddy, whatever the assignment sheet says. On multi-shift sites, match the pairing to the shift pattern first, then confirm everything else.

Does the buddy have to be the most senior person available?

No, and it's usually the wrong choice if seniority is the only reason. Patience, proximity and genuine willingness matter more. The most senior person is often the busiest, and a buddy who doesn't have the time ends up being a buddy in name only.

What if a new starter has done this exact job somewhere else before?

The pace through unfamiliar tasks can move faster, but the compliance sign-off checklist never gets shortened. Experience elsewhere confirms general competence, not that they know this site's version of the standard. Skip the wrong thing here and it tends to surface later as an incident, not as a saved afternoon.

How do we know if the induction actually happened, or if the checklist just got filled in at the end?

Look at the dates, not just whether every line is ticked. Genuine day-by-day induction leaves sign-offs spread across the fortnight, dated as things actually happened. A checklist that arrives fully signed on the last day, all one date, usually means it was closed out rather than lived. See the guide's operating condition in section 3.
