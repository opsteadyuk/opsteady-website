Maintenance Response System

Restore equipment quickly without normalising repeat failures. When a breakdown happens, it isn't always the failure itself that costs the most time.

£179Buy now
Overview

Often it's what happens next: nobody's quite sure who to call first, the part that's needed is somewhere nobody can immediately locate, and there's no clear point at which the issue stops being handled informally and gets escalated to someone with more authority or resource. The breakdown might have been unavoidable. The hour spent figuring out how to respond to it usually wasn't.

At a glance
Time
Half a day to map the response path for the single highest-impact asset, once real downtime data on response time, not just frequency, is in hand.
Who's involved
Whoever's actually called when something breaks, maintenance or otherwise. Whoever has authority to bring in outside support once the escalation trigger fires. Whoever owns Downtime Tracking's data, since response time needs to be visible there to confirm this is the right branch.
What you'll need
Downtime Tracking's data, specifically showing response time as the dominant issue, not breakdown count. Real detail from a recent breakdown, not a theoretical best case, since the actual delays are usually more informative than anyone expects.
