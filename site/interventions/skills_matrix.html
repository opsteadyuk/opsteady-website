Common questions

FAQ

What's actually different about the ILUO levels versus just rating someone 1 to 4?

This is worth getting right, not just a labelling choice. A number scale invites a guess: is this person a "3" because they seem competent, or because someone counted their years on the job? ILUO is behaviourally defined instead: I means briefed and working alongside a qualified person, not yet unsupervised; L means working to the standard unassisted under normal conditions, confirmed by at least two observed performances on separate days; U means fully independent including at least one abnormal condition, with the person able to say what they'd do in the two most likely failure situations; O means having trained someone else to L or above using the standard, with a clean quality and safety record. The test for any rating: can you point to the specific, observed evidence that earned it. If not, it's an opinion, not a level. See guide section 13 and the working file's Assessment criteria tab, which is the definitive version of these criteria.

Why does a zero on Owners for a task matter, or does it always matter?

It doesn't always matter, and treating every zero the same is the mistake. Check the contingency register first. If the task has a current vendor, a real cost, and a stated lead time, someone has already made an informed decision that outsourcing beats training an Owner in-house, and that's a defensible choice, not a hidden risk. If there's no contingency entry at all, it's a genuine single point of failure sitting unexamined. See guide section 16 and the working file's Contingency tab.

Do I need a full Set Standards register before I can start this?

Not a complete one, but you do need a standard to rate people against for whatever tasks you're building the matrix for. Ratings without a defined standard behind them are opinions with a letter attached. If Set Standards is only partly built, start the matrix on the tasks that already have a written standard, and add more as Set Standards catches up. See guide section 4.

How is this different from Training Plan & Competency Sign-off?

They're not the same tool, and it's worth being precise about the boundary. This module owns the assessment mechanic itself: what counts as evidence for each ILUO transition, and the dated sign-off record. Training Plan & Competency Sign-off's job is turning this matrix's gap list into a scheduled, resourced plan, names and dates against red cells, not re-doing the rating. If you're asking "is this person qualified," that's here. If you're asking "when is someone getting trained on this," that's the other module. See guide section 10.

Won't this just turn into a ranking people compete over?

It can, if capability and performance aren't kept genuinely separate. The moment this matrix touches pay or appraisal, marking goes kind and the whole record stops being honest, which is worse than not having one. If you notice it starting to read as a league table, that's the moment to say so directly, not something to let settle in quietly. See guide section 9 and section 17.
