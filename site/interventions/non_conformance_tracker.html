Overview

A single non-conformance always looks like a one-off — whoever catches it deals with it and moves on, because dealing with the part in front of them is the actual job. Nobody's job, most of the time, is to notice that the same one-off has happened 11 times this month, scattered across 3 shifts, three inspectors' private notebooks and one supervisor's memory. When right-first-time drops and someone asks why, the honest answer is that nobody can say, because no single record ever held all of it in one place.

This module fixes that with the simplest possible structure: log every non-conformance as it happens, even roughly categorised, and rank the log monthly by both frequency and cost, since the two don't always point at the same defect. The working file is one connected workbook — an NC log, an editable Categories tab, a calculated Pareto tab (with a "minimum entries before this feels representative" setting, defaulting to 20), and a six-month Trend view — so the pattern that was invisible scattered across three notebooks becomes obvious the moment it's all in one column.

Done properly, the top one or two categories get handed to Root Cause Analysis by name, with the data behind them, not left as a pile nobody's acted on — and the tracker's own Round-maturity verdict (Established / Not yet established) is what tells Poka-Yoke & Andon (P3.3) whether there's a recurring defect worth mistake-proofing yet.

At a glance
Time

An hour to agree defect categories and set the log up. After that, logging itself takes seconds per entry, not a separate task.

Who's involved

Whoever's currently catching non-conformances day to day — inspectors, operators, whoever's actually there when something's found — and whoever owns Root Cause Analysis, since they're the destination for whatever this log surfaces. For your own site, decide: who logs a non-conformance, whoever finds it or a nominated person per shift; who reviews the Pareto monthly and decides what goes to RCA; and who can change the defect category list once it's agreed, and how often.

What you'll need

The NC Log, Pareto & Trend workbook (Excel), supplied with this module. A working or emerging OTIF & Right-First-Time Dashboard, or at minimum a live concern that RFT is dropping — a finished dashboard isn't required to start. A simple, agreed list of defect categories, not perfect, agreed, which goes straight onto the workbook's own Categories tab.

Before you start

Start logging today, even with rough categories, rather than waiting for a perfect taxonomy that never quite gets finished — three months of roughly categorised data starting today is worth more than three months of properly categorised data starting next quarter. Tell everyone who'll be logging, so the first entry isn't a surprise, and tell whoever owns Root Cause Analysis, since the monthly handoff depends on them expecting it.

If some version of a log already exists, scattered across notebooks or individual spreadsheets, pull every existing record into one place first, even where categories don't match — reconciling categories after consolidation is easier than standardising before anyone's agreed to log centrally at all.
