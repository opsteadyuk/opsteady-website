Common questions

FAQ

What if we don't know the cause yet when we're logging something?

Log the defect precisely and mark the cause unknown. Don't guess a plausible-sounding cause just to fill the field, that quietly corrupts the trend and is harder to catch later than a blank field is. The pattern across many entries is what points toward root cause, not any single guess made under time pressure. See guide section 5.

What if the categories we picked turn out to be wrong, or too broad?

Watch the "other" category. If it starts growing, that's the signal to revisit the category list, not a reason to log less carefully. Categories don't need to be perfect at the start, they need revisiting once they stop being useful. See guide section 5 and section 9.

Do we need a finished Right-First-Time Dashboard before we start this?

No, a live concern that RFT is dropping is enough to start. The dashboard and this tracker can develop alongside each other. See guide section 4.

Should we rank by how often a defect happens, or by what it costs?

Both, and check whether they agree. A low-cost defect that happens constantly and a rare defect that scraps a full batch can both deserve attention, for different reasons. Where the two rankings genuinely disagree: safety or compliance risk overrides both rankings first, always; short of that, weigh how big the gap actually is (a runaway cost outlier usually beats a narrow frequency lead), how much capacity Root Cause Analysis genuinely has this cycle, and which candidate is trending worse. Decide with those in mind, not by picking a rule to have one. See guide section 3 and section 14.

What happens to the top defect type once it's identified?

It gets handed to Root Cause Analysis by name, with the data behind it. If that handoff isn't actually happening, the same defect type will keep sitting at the top of the Pareto month after month with nothing changing, which is a broken process, not a hard problem. See guide section 16.
