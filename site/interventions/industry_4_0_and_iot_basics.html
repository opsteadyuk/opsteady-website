Industry 4.0 & IoT Basics

Industry 4.0 and IoT get treated two ways on most sites, chased as buzzwords because a competitor has them or a board wants to look modern, or dismissed as something only large, capital-rich sites can use. Both miss the actual question, which has nothing to do with site size. It's whether connecting a specific piece of equipment would tell you something you genuinely need to know sooner than you find out today.

Common questions

FAQ

We're a small site. Is this even relevant to us?

Yes, since the value-check has nothing to do with site size. It asks whether one specific asset, chosen by you, would tell you something worth knowing sooner than it does today, and whether that's worth what connecting it costs. A small site with one genuine candidate gets exactly as much value from running this properly as a large one does. See guide section 1 and section 15, failure 4.

How is this different from Going Digital (E3)?

Going Digital asks whether a paper tracker should become digital. This module is the same discipline, one level up, applied to physical connected equipment, sensors, condition monitoring, rather than paper. Going Digital's basic judgement habit is worth having in place first. See guide section 9.

What counts as "the one thing worth knowing sooner"?

A specific, named question tied to a real decision, not general visibility. "We'd know more" isn't it. "We'd catch a temperature drift within minutes instead of finding it at the next scheduled check, hours later" is. If nobody can name the actual decision the data would change, the candidate isn't ready yet. See guide section 4 and section 17.

Our board wants to see investment in this. What do we do if we don't have a strong candidate yet?

Run the value-check on your best real candidate rather than picking something for visibility. A proportionate, evidence-based first case, chosen properly, gives a board something genuine to point to, and protects the site from a purchase that doesn't earn its place. See guide section 16.

What if the value-check comes back genuinely marginal, not a clear yes or no?

Check whether a regulated-sector documentation benefit applies, a continuously logged record can be worth real weight in an audit or customer inspection, even where the operational case alone is borderline. If it's still unclear after that, write the reasoning down and set it aside as a documented "not yet," not a closed door. See guide section 13 and section 17.
