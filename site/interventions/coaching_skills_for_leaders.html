Coaching Skills for Leaders

Most leaders on a manufacturing floor operate in one of two modes when something needs fixing: tell the person exactly what to do, or step in and do it themselves because it's faster than explaining. Both feel efficient in the moment. Both quietly cost the site the same thing over time: skill stays concentrated in the leader, the same problem reappears because the person who hit it never worked out why, and the leader becomes the bottleneck every decision has to pass through, because nobody else has been given the chance to build the judgement to make it themselves.

Common questions

FAQ

What if I already know the answer, is it dishonest to ask anyway?

No, provided the question is genuinely open rather than a test with one acceptable answer in mind. Asking "what's slowing you down" when you suspect the cause isn't dishonest, it gives the person the chance to confirm, correct, or add something you hadn't considered. It becomes dishonest, or at least pointless, only when the question has exactly one "right" answer you're fishing for. See Pro section 15's second failure.

What if the person just says "I don't know" when I ask?

That's a genuine answer, not a failed question, and it's worth sitting with rather than immediately supplying your own view. A short silence, or a follow-up like "what's your best guess" or "what would you try first," often gets further than filling the gap yourself. If it genuinely stalls, section 13's sequencing variant covers offering your own expertise after their attempt, not instead of it.

How is this different from a monthly one-to-one?

A monthly one-to-one is better than nothing, but it's a separate, slower mechanism. This module builds coaching into moments that already happen daily or weekly, the job instruction, the board, a Gemba walk, so the skill gets used far more often and stays connected to real, current events rather than something recalled a few weeks later. See Pro section 16.

What if a leader is naturally more directive and finds this hard?

That's common, often among the most technically capable leaders, since they can see the answer instantly and it feels inefficient not to just say it. The fix isn't removing their expertise from the conversation, it's sequencing it: let the person attempt their own diagnosis first, then offer the leader's own view afterward. See Pro section 13.

Do we need Culture (F3) in place before starting this?

At least partially, yes. A genuine question only lands as coaching, rather than suspicious interrogation, when people already trust the site's response to problems is fair. If Culture isn't yet in some working use, be cautious about rolling this out broadly, and treat Culture as the more urgent priority. See section 4's minimum decision fork.
