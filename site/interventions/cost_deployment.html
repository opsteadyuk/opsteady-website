Cost Deployment

This module takes the losses and wastes a site already knows about, downtime, scrap, overtime, quality escapes, and turns them into a single ranked view: what each major loss category costs the site per year, roughly, its trend, who owns it, and which method or module is the primary way to attack it. It's the bridge from "we know the losses" to "we have a ranked, owned cost-reduction agenda," not a new way of measuring loss, but a way of putting existing measurements into one shared currency.

What it takes

Time
Building the first-pass matrix typically takes a few hours once the underlying loss data already exists; most of the effort is in pulling together numbers that already live in separate places, not generating new data.
Who's involved
Whoever owns the operational side of prioritisation, usually a plant or operations manager, plus access to someone finance-literate who can sanity-check the cost estimates against the real P&L.
Materials
Existing loss data: the Waste & Cost-of-Quality Calculator's output, OEE loss trees, downtime logs, scrap records, overtime tracking, and customer complaint costs where available. No specialist costing software is required for the standard version of this module.

Common questions

FAQ

Is this the same as a full Activity-Based Costing system?

No. This module is a practical, first-pass ranking built from data the site already has, not a full ABC system or a corporate cost-deployment programme. Pro covers a lightweight allocation approach for multi-product or shared-service sites where losses are hard to attribute, without requiring the full formal system. See Pro and Essentials section 2 and Pro section 11.

What if we don't have good cost data for some loss categories yet?

Build a rough, clearly caveated first-pass estimate for those categories rather than waiting for perfect data. A roughly-right full picture is more useful early on than one precise figure sitting next to several blanks. Refine the estimate on the next scheduled refresh once better data exists. See Pro and Essentials section 5's minimum decision fork.

Who should challenge the cost estimates before they're published?

Someone genuinely finance or P&L-literate, named specifically, whose job is to sanity-check the numbers against the real statement, not simply approve whatever operations produces. No matrix should be published without this step. See Pro and Essentials section 4 and Pro section 16's third failure.

Does every loss category need to be costed to the pound?

No, and trying to do that can actually be counterproductive. What matters most is that the ranking order between categories would likely survive a more careful recalculation, not pinpoint precision on any single figure. Presenting rough estimates as if they were exact tends to make disputes harder to resolve honestly later. See Pro section 15's judgement calls and section 16's seventh failure.

What happens if the highest-ranked loss doesn't currently have an owner?

Treat that as a standing escalation, not a detail to sort out eventually. An unowned top-ranked loss is exactly the kind of gap this module exists to surface, and naming an owner is the immediate next step, not a reason to leave the category off the matrix. See Pro section 16's fifth failure and section 17's fourth awkward conversation.
