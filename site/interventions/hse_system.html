HSE System

Every statutory health, safety and environmental obligation covered, current, and owned, not scattered across whoever last dealt with an inspector, and not quietly bypassed by whatever's changing elsewhere on site.

Overview

Almost no site starts from genuinely nothing — a risk assessment somewhere, a fire safety plan of some kind, a folder of certificates. Sites rarely fail here because nothing exists; they fail because what exists is scattered, isn't kept current, and, most commonly, isn't connected to whatever's changing elsewhere on site. A layout change, an updated SOP, a rebalanced line can each carry HSE consequences nobody thinks to check, precisely because whoever's making the change is thinking about output, flow or quality, not compliance.

This module is a register, not a safety programme: every statutory obligation the site carries, who owns each one, what evidence proves it's met, and when it's next due — a compliance record that actually holds up when someone asks to see it. It's deliberately narrow: Opsteady isn't a health and safety consultancy, this doesn't try to define or run an HSE programme, and where a site already has a dedicated HSE team or a mature register, this module's role shifts from building to connecting, making sure their work is visibly linked to change happening elsewhere on site. It sits next to, not inside, Safety Observation & Near-Miss System (E9.2): this owns the compliance and systems layer, that owns the daily behavioural layer, and the threshold for a near miss escalating into this register's formal record is a site-set decision, not a fixed rule.

Done properly, every obligation on the register carries current evidence, a named owner, and no overdue review sitting without a stated reason — reviewed monthly as a whole, with any change landing from elsewhere on site checked against it before being called finished.

This module is not legal advice and doesn't replace competent HSE advice. The specific regulations, categories and thresholds it references — RIDDOR's reporting categories, LOLER's examination intervals, environmental permit and trade effluent conditions — are set and revised by the relevant regulator (HSE, the Environment Agency, your water company, your local authority), not by Opsteady; confirm current requirements before relying on anything here for an actual compliance decision.

At a glance
Time

Building the first version of the register, for a standard single site, is a half-day to a day of focused work, most of it gathering evidence already held rather than creating anything new. Closing gaps that surface takes longer and varies by what's found.

Who's involved

One person owns the register day to day — not automatically the same person as the site's legally responsible person for fire safety, a specific named role under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, and the two should be confirmed separately rather than assumed to be the same. The Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 also requires access to competent health and safety advice, in-house or external, for anything beyond the genuinely straightforward; this module assumes that advice is available to draw on, not that it replaces it.

What you'll need

Whatever evidence already exists: risk assessments, COSHH sheets, equipment certificates, permits, insurance documentation — most sites have more of this than they think, just not in one place. The working file, split across two files: E9.1_Obligations_Register_and_Calendar.xlsx (an Obligations register tab — status worked out from the date, no row accepted without both an owner and a review date, an obligation with no evidence named as an immediate priority — and a Review calendar tab showing what falls due each month and what's already overdue going into it) and HSE_System_Working_File.md (a RIDDOR quick-reference, an improvement-change quick-reference, a starting checklist of common statutory obligations, and standard review cycles, each pointing to hse.gov.uk for current figures rather than reproducing them as fixed).

Before you start

If a register, even an informal one, already exists somewhere on site, don't rebuild it from nothing — cross-check it against the working file's checklist and close whatever gaps that comparison surfaces, building fresh only where nothing usable already exists. Start with whichever obligation is closest to its review date, or has no evidence at all, not the whole list at once — and if you genuinely don't know who your site's responsible person for fire safety is, confirm that first, since it's often the fastest live gap to close.

Your actual statutory obligations depend on your sector, substances, equipment and activities — a site with no lifting equipment has no LOLER obligation, a site with an environmental permit has conditions this guide can't predict — so treat the working file's starting checklist as a base to adapt, not a finished list to copy.
