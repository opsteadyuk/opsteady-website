Do you recognise this?

One part of the line is always the problem, and you're not sure it's the one everyone keeps blaming. Lead times keep creeping up even though every station looks busy.

Why it persists

Without a measured answer, the loudest station gets the attention, not necessarily the one actually holding output back. Fixing the wrong station doesn't move output. It just moves the argument.

Why the obvious response can be wrong

A busy station isn't automatically the constraint. High utilisation on work that isn't the real hold-up can look like the answer and not be it. Before spending on new equipment, overtime, or headcount, it's worth being certain which step it would actually fix.

What needs to be established first

Which single step is genuinely setting the pace of the whole line, measured, not guessed. Then what that's actually costing you in units per week.
