Common questions

How long do I actually need to log for?
A week is the minimum, not a guarantee. If your output genuinely varies by day of week or shift pattern, one week can point at the wrong step. Ask yourself: would a different week plausibly show a different constraint? If the honest answer is yes, log two weeks before naming anything.
What if my constraint moves during the week?
That's a real finding on multi-product or multi-shift sites, not a measurement error. Don't force a single answer onto the summary sheet. Note both candidates, run one more full week, and let the pattern settle. If it keeps moving in step with what you're producing, that's a high-mix pattern, run the analysis on your highest-volume or highest-pain product family first, since a blended average across your whole mix hides the real answer.
Do I have to buy new equipment once I've found my constraint?
No, and buying too fast is one of the most common mistakes with this tool. Before any capacity request, work through the exploit and subordinate checklist: get everything the constraint can already give you (no avoidable stops, no scrap, no waiting on material, priority for shared resources), and deliberately pace every other station to the constraint's speed rather than letting them run flat-out. Most sites find real, free gains here. Only take a genuine, evidenced shortfall to Capacity Planning Model, not the whole original gap.
Why would I deliberately slow a station down that could run faster?
Because running it faster doesn't get you a single extra unit out the door, the constraint still sets the pace regardless. It usually causes real harm instead: work piles up faster than the constraint can absorb it, or a shared resource, a setter, a tool, a supervisor's attention, gets pulled away from the constraint to keep the faster station fed. This is the part of the tool people push back on hardest, and it's worth explaining plainly rather than just asserting it, see the guide's awkward conversations section for the full version.
What if the constraint turns out to be a person, not a machine?
The tool doesn't care what shape the constraint takes. If the honest finding is that one person's pace sets the line's ceiling, that's a real finding, and it points toward cross-training or standard work, not toward blame. Naming it accurately is this tool's job. What you do about it is a separate, calmer conversation.
