A real operational problem

Everyone can point at the bottleneck. It's rarely the right one.

Ask a room of experienced people where the constraint is and you'll get a confident, specific answer — usually the loudest or most recent station, rarely checked against real throughput data. The station nobody's watching, running fine but sitting in front of a two-day queue nobody's measured, is often costing more than the one everyone talks about.

What you're likely seeing

Do these sound familiar?

  • Every production meeting names the same station as "the problem," and the fix requested is the same each time.

  • A capacity or headcount request has been raised more than once for the same station, without resolving the delay.

  • Nobody has actually logged throughput or cycle time by step — the belief comes from who's mentioned most often when a shipment runs late.

  • Work-in-progress is visibly piling up somewhere that isn't the station everyone blames.

What might actually be happening

The loudest station and the real constraint are often two different places

This persists because "busy" and "the constraint" aren't the same thing. A station that runs fast but only in batches can still be the real limit, because the wait to form a batch counts. High utilisation on the wrong work looks like the answer and isn't. Free advice can explain that in general — it can't watch your specific line and tell you where yours actually sits.

Before you buy anything

Check these first

  • Has this line ever had a real, measured constraint analysis run on it — not just an opinion? If it's been months, a fixed constraint moves on, and the old answer may already be wrong.

  • Is the suspected problem capacity, or reliability? If a station is stopping unpredictably rather than simply running slow, that's a downtime problem, not a capacity one — a different starting point.

  • Do you have at least a week of real throughput or cycle-time data by step? If not, that's the actual first move, not a capital request.
