Overview

Most sites already quote a schedule number, and most of those numbers are quietly answering only one of three different questions. Attainment asks whether you made the volume you planned, and it can hit 100% while you built the wrong things. Production adherence asks whether you made the right items in the right quantities, capped so overproducing an easy line can't hide underproducing an awkward one. Timing adherence asks whether it happened when the plan said it would. A site can be strong on one of these and quietly failing on another, and a single blended number hides exactly which.

This tool reads all three from one log, so a slip shows up as what it actually is rather than getting averaged away. It measures adherence to a plan, it doesn't produce one: it sits downstream of Bottleneck Analysis Tool (P4.1) and Capacity Planning Model (P4.3), and building it against a guessed schedule rather than one built on the real constraint means tracking adherence to the wrong thing. Every miss also gets a real cause logged against it, not just a mark, which builds into a Pareto over successive review periods showing where the fix actually belongs — a floor-side cause like changeover overrun, or back with whoever owns the plan if "reschedule or priority change" keeps dominating.

Done well, all three numbers are read together every review against a target the site set for itself, and a rising "Other" share in the miss-cause breakdown is the earliest warning that logging has gone shallow, well before the headline numbers show any change.

At a glance
Time

Half a day to set your definitions, then a few minutes a day logging as jobs complete.

Who's involved

Whoever owns the schedule — a planner, an ops manager, or you, if that's the same person on your site. For your own site, decide: who currently owns this number, if anyone, and who needs telling before you start, particularly if a schedule number already gets reported upward.

What you'll need

The workbook, and access to whatever holds your plan and your actuals — an ERP export, a whiteboard, a spreadsheet you already run. Bottleneck Analysis Tool's confirmed constraint and Capacity Planning Model's own plan, since this tool measures adherence to that plan rather than producing it.

Before you start

If a schedule number already gets reported upward, tell whoever receives that report before you touch the definition. Open the Definitions tab before logging a single job: decide what you're measuring against — the original schedule (harder to hit, more honest about what the plan actually promised) or the latest approved reschedule (easier to hit, easier to flatter without meaning to) — and set the tolerance window as a whole number of days. If time is genuinely short, timing adherence on its own is a legitimate place to start; add production adherence once the daily habit holds, then attainment for the full picture.
