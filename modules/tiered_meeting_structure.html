Tiered Meeting Structure

The right problem solved at the right level, first time.

Overview

Most sites have too many meetings and too little structure behind them. Some problems get raised in five different rooms before anyone actually decides what to do about them; others never get raised anywhere that can act on them, and sit unresolved at the bottom for months. Without a defined structure, what reaches senior leaders is decided by whoever escalates loudest, most often, or most recently — not by what actually needs their attention.

This module fixes that with a small number of tiers, sized to what the site's management layers actually are, most sites need three (team, area, site), each with a fixed time box, a fixed agenda tied directly to the SQDC board — the board is the agenda, not a separate thing anyone checks alongside it — and a specific, testable escalation rule agreed in advance rather than judged in the moment. Where Shift Handover System is running, its log feeds straight into Tier 1's own board review.

Done properly, Tier 1 runs to time daily for two weeks straight, escalations move up only when they genuinely meet the stated criteria, and — over time — most items close at Tier 1, a smaller number genuinely reach Tier 2, and only the rare capital or cross-area decision reaches Tier 3, freeing senior time for things only senior people can decide.

At a glance
Time

A day to map existing meetings and design the tier structure. Longer to actually retire the old meetings it replaces.

Who's involved

Whoever chairs each proposed tier. The SQDC board owner, since the board becomes the agenda. Whoever currently owns the meetings this structure will replace, since retiring their meeting is part of the build, not a side effect of it. For your own site, decide: who has final say on how many tiers this site needs; who chairs each tier, and whether that's fixed or rotates; and who owns the escalation criteria, and who can change them once they're set.

What you'll need

The SQDC board in some working form, since it's this structure's own agenda. Shift Handover System in some working form too, since its log feeds directly into Tier 1's own board review. An honest list of every recurring meeting that currently exists on this site, with who attends and how long each genuinely runs.

Before you start

Map what already exists before designing what should exist — most sites are surprised how many meetings are already running once they're all listed in one place, and several usually turn out to be duplicating each other under different names. Tell everyone whose current meeting is being replaced, before it disappears from their calendar — a meeting that just stops without explanation reads as things being decided without them. Get the SQDC board into at least a working form first if it isn't already, since a tier structure with no board behind it drifts back into a generic status update fairly quickly.
