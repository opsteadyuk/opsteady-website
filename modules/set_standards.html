Overview

A site can have standards, SOPs and a floor that looks in good order, while every operator does the job their own way and gets a different result — the paperwork says one thing, the night shift does another (What this is, and what it fixes). That gap persists on sites that already know the theory because a standard written at a desk, describing the ideal version of a job, looks better in an audit than one that admits the floor has its own workarounds, and nobody's job is to notice the drift until something goes wrong and the gap becomes visible the hard way (What this is, and what it fixes).

This module replaces desk-written procedures with a four-step capture method — watch the job done by the person who does it best, write what actually happens, read it back to them at the machine, and correct it until they say "that's it" (Build it). A Standards Register tracks, process by process, whether a standard exists, whether it's been confirmed against current practice, and whether it's actually consulted when something goes wrong, and ranks the gaps automatically, worst first, so nothing has to be judged by eye (The working file).

Done properly, a captured and confirmed standard becomes the thing people actually reach for when something breaks, not a document that only satisfies an auditor (How you know it is working; The two or three ways this dies). It's also the foundation two other modules depend on: rating someone on the Skills Matrix against a standard that doesn't exist isn't a real rating, and a Layered Process Audit needs something to check the floor against rather than an assumption (Works with).

At a glance
Time

About 1 hour per process to capture it properly — watching the job, writing it, reading it back, correcting it until the operator confirms it — plus about 30 minutes to set up the register itself. After that, holding it costs about 15 minutes per process every 6 months, plus whatever time a fix already takes, since updating the standard happens inside that time rather than alongside it (Before you start).

Who's involved

One named owner for the register — not a committee (Before you start). The operator who does each job best, for the capture walk (Before you start). Whoever signs off standards as correct at your site, who may or may not be the same person as the owner (Before you start). No specific title is required to lead the capture itself — what's required is standing to ask an experienced operator to slow down for about an hour and be watched, and enough follow-through to actually update the register afterwards.

What you'll need

The Standards Register (Excel, four tabs) and the SOP Template (Word), both supplied with this module. A notebook or tablet for the capture walk. Nothing else (Before you start; The working file).

Before you start

Decide what earns a standard using three risk questions: does getting it wrong hurt someone, scrap the product or fail a downstream check, or damage the asset — a yes to any one means it needs a standard (Before you start). One override: if the job is done differently depending on who does it, and that changes the result, it needs a standard regardless of how it scores. One exclusion: if it's genuinely obvious and the result never varies, leave it (Before you start). Set up the register first — about 30 minutes — so you have a list to work through rather than a blank page, then capture the worst, most inconsistent process first rather than the easiest one (Before you start). Decide, for your own site, who signs off a standard, who may update it between reviews, who's told when it changes, who owns the register day to day, and who's consulted before a standard is retired (Before you start).
