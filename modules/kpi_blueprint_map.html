KPI Blueprint

Know why a number moved in minutes, not a week of digging: one page mapping the one number your site lives or dies on, down to something a named person can change this shift.

Overview

Most sites measure plenty already — boards, reports, an existing KPI tree — but can't say why a number moved without a week of digging, because the numbers sit in silos and the links between layers live only in experienced people's heads. That gap gets paid for twice: once in management time spent reconstructing a cause that should take minutes to find, and once in decisions, because when tracing is slow the loudest problem gets the attention rather than the biggest one.

The KPI Blueprint Map takes the one number a site genuinely lives or dies on and maps, on one page, what feeds it down through four fixed layers — Business, Site, Process, Floor. It's built on paper first, in about ninety minutes, with someone who watches the numbers daily, not alone at a desk. Every link carries a confidence mark (known, assumed, or unknown), and the bottom layer has to pass the floor test — can a named person on the floor see that measure and change it this shift — or the tree hasn't actually reached the floor.

The result is a working map, not a diagram: a specific route to trace when a number misses, instead of a week of reconstructing the cause from memory, and a scoped, honest list of exactly which links are still assumed or unknown rather than a vague sense that "we need better data."

At a glance
Time

About ninety minutes for the first tree — forty minutes on paper with one other person, the rest in the workbook.

Who's involved

You, plus at least one person who watches the numbers daily — building this alone in an office is the single most common way it goes wrong. Decide, for your site: who decides the top-of-tree number (usually you, sometimes set above you); who can add or remove a branch once the tree exists; who gets told when the tree changes; and who needs to agree before a measure's owner changes.

What you'll need

The tree canvas (printed), a pen, and the workbook.

Before you start

Run the paper session first, standing up if possible, before anyone touches the spreadsheet — done in one sitting, not spread across a week, since momentum matters more than polish on a first pass. If the site already has something calling itself a KPI tree, treat this as an audit of that existing version rather than a fresh build: bring it to the session and check every link against the floor test as a claim to test, not a fact to copy.
