5S Audit Pack

The formal, scored layer underneath your daily visual check, kept deliberately separate from it, so both do the job the other can't.

Overview

If Visual Management Foundations is already running, a daily stranger-test check already catches whether things look right today. This module is a different tool, not a bigger version of that one: a formal, scored, periodic audit across Sort, Set in Order, Shine, Standardise and Sustain, each scored and tracked over time. Blending the two into one tool is a documented, common reason 5S programmes stall — the daily glance catches drift early, the scored audit builds the historical record that shows whether the whole system is actually holding, and points at exactly which of the five is weakest.

Scoring is deliberately count-based wherever possible — how many unnecessary items, how many missing from their defined location — so two auditors scoring the same area independently should land on the same number, with only a handful of percentage- or judgement-based questions needing a shared call agreed up front. Frequency tracks maturity rather than staying fixed: weekly for the first 90 days to catch drift before it becomes the new normal, stretching to monthly and then quarterly once scores hold steady. Sustain scoring lowest of the five is the field's most consistent finding across 5S programmes, and it's a design reason, not a discipline failure — the other four S's produce a visible artefact to point to, Sustain is the absence of visible failure, which is far easier to let slip.

Done properly, every low score carries a real note naming what actually drove it, not the score repeated in words, and — the genuine test — Sustain starts closing the gap with the other four across successive audits, the sign that the checking rhythm between formal audits is actually holding.

At a glance
Time

Half a day to agree your scale and areas, then 20 to 30 minutes per area per audit.

Who's involved

A small cross-functional group works better than one auditor working alone, since different eyes catch different drift. For your own site, decide: which areas you'll audit, and how often to start; who's on the audit team, and whether it's genuinely cross-functional; and who owns turning a low score into a named action with a date.

What you'll need

The Opsteady 5S Sustain Audit Form for scoring on the floor, this workbook for logging the subtotals afterwards, and a camera or phone for photos to back up scores. The working file ships as two files: P6.1_5S_Audit_Workbook.xlsx (three tabs — Definitions, Audit Log, Dashboard) and P6.1_5S_Sustain_Audit_Form.xlsx (twenty questions, four per S, each scored 0 to 4 against a written expectation, each carrying its own Action created flag).

Before you start

Agree your scale, areas and starting frequency in writing before the first audit, and tell everyone whose area gets audited beforehand, framed as finding gaps in the system, not finding fault. Written expectations for what "normal" looks like in each area need to exist before scoring starts, ideally from Visual Standards & Workplace Organisation Guide (P6.2) — scoring compliance to a standard that was never defined isn't meaningful.

Confirm first whether this is a first audit on an area or a repeat: a first audit scores honestly against the written expectation for an accurate baseline, not a good result; a repeat audit scores the same way, then compares the new subtotal against the last one on the Dashboard, checking specifically whether last time's low-scoring questions got their corrective action.
