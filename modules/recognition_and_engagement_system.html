Recognition & Engagement System

Noticing what actually happened, close to when it happened — a simple, leader-owned practice that makes specific contribution visible instead of waiting for the annual event.

Overview

Most sites recognise people twice a year: an annual awards event, and maybe a mention in a newsletter nobody reads closely. In between, the moments that actually deserve noticing pass without comment, and the person who did it either assumes nobody noticed, or correctly concludes that nobody did. This happens because recognition feels like a soft skill, easy to deprioritise against the next production issue, so it defaults to whatever's fastest — a generic "well done team," or nothing at all. Generic praise costs a leader almost nothing and gives the receiver almost nothing back, since it doesn't tell them what specifically they did right or prove anyone actually watched.

This module is a simple, leader-owned practice for noticing specific contribution in the flow of work, close to when it happened — not an HR engagement programme, not an annual survey, not a points scheme. It sits downstream of Culture (F3): recognition only lands as recognition, rather than as exposure or favouritism, when people already trust that the site's response to problems is fair. The build itself is light — agree what kinds of contribution count (results, but also behaviours like catching a problem early or helping across teams), give leaders a simple prompt, create a visible log or board, practise on real events, and run a light monthly scan for two things: is recognition spreading beyond a narrow group, and is it staying specific rather than drifting back to generic.

Done well, recognition is happening weekly without a campaign pushing it, it's specific rather than generic, and it's reaching more than a narrow group of people.

At a glance
Time

The practice itself takes almost no extra time once it's a habit — a specific comment takes seconds longer than a generic one. Building the habit takes a first week of deliberate practice (three real entries from one leader), and the monthly scan afterwards takes a few minutes.

Who's involved

One leader to start, ideally someone willing to practise this visibly so others can see what it looks like before it's asked of them too. For your own site, decide: which leader starts first and who else needs to see what good recognition looks like before being asked to do it themselves; where the log or board lives and who can see it; who does the light monthly scan for patterns and who acts on what it finds; and, on a multi-shift site, how recognition given on one shift stays visible to leaders on another.

What you'll need

A simple recognition prompt card and a visible log or board — which can be as basic as a shared sheet or a corner of an existing team board. The supplied working file, P1.8_Recognition_Prompt_and_Log.xlsx, holds both as two tabs.

Before you start

Agree explicitly, before starting, what recognition is not: not a substitute for fair pay, not a way to paper over a broken process that keeps generating heroics to recognise — if the same person keeps getting recognised for catching the same recurring problem, that's a signal for Root Cause Analysis or the relevant pillar module, not a reason to recognise them again. Don't launch this as a site-wide initiative on day one — start with one leader practising on real events, review whether it landed the way it was meant to, then extend it. Check first whether Culture (F3) is already in some working use on the site: if not, start with recognition logged privately to the individual rather than made visible, since without that baseline being singled out can feel exposing rather than positive.
