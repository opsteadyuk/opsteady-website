Coaching Skills for Leaders

A short, practical set of questions a leader can use inside moments that already happen — the job instruction, the board, a Gemba walk — that develops people in the flow of work instead of only directing or correcting after the fact.

Overview

Most leaders on a manufacturing floor default to one of two moves when something needs fixing: tell the person exactly what to do, or step in and do it themselves because it's faster than explaining. Both feel efficient in the moment. Both quietly cost the site the same thing over time — skill stays concentrated in the leader, the same problem reappears because the person who hit it never worked out why, and the leader becomes the bottleneck every decision has to pass through. This happens because telling is fast and, under production pressure, fast wins — most leaders were never shown the difference between telling and coaching, or given a simple way to do it inside an already-busy shift.

This module is not a generic coaching-for-managers course and produces no qualification. It's a short, repeatable question sequence — confirm the standard both people already understand, ask a genuine open question, listen for the real obstacle, agree one next step owned by the person being coached, set a specific follow-up — deliberately small enough to fit inside a two-minute exchange at a workstation. It depends on two things already being in at least some use: a routine like Supervisor Standard Work or a Gemba rhythm that creates natural coaching moments, and Culture's (F3) response discipline, since a genuine question only lands as coaching rather than suspicious interrogation where people already trust the site's response to problems is fair.

Done well, coaching conversations happen inside routines that already exist without a special campaign, the person being coached can state the next step they themselves own, and at least one skill or behaviour measurably improves as a result.

At a glance
Time

Almost none extra once it's a habit — a coaching exchange typically adds a couple of minutes to a moment that was going to happen anyway. Building the habit takes a first week of deliberate practice: each participating leader runs three short coaching conversations, then reviews one with a peer or their own leader.

Who's involved

Leaders who already have natural coaching moments in their routine — supervisors, team leaders, anyone running standard work or a Gemba walk. For your own site, decide: which leaders start first, and whether they already have a routine that creates natural coaching moments; who observes and gives feedback on early coaching attempts, a peer or the leader's own manager; where a genuinely good coaching example from your own site gets captured and shared; and who checks, over time, whether coaching is reaching everyone a leader manages or only the people they find easiest to talk to.

What you'll need

The coaching prompt card and a light practice log for anyone actively building the skill — both delivered in P1.10_Coaching_Prompt_and_Practice_Log.xlsx.

Before you start

Confirm two things are already in at least some use: a routine such as Supervisor Standard Work or a Gemba rhythm, so natural coaching moments actually exist to use, and Culture's response discipline, at least partially, so a genuine question doesn't land as interrogation. If neither exists yet, establish the routine first, or start with whatever reliable moment does exist and treat building the fuller routine as a parallel priority rather than a hard prerequisite. Start with leaders who already have a standard routine, pair them for short observed coaching exchanges with feedback, and deliberately capture one or two examples of genuinely good coaching from your own site.
