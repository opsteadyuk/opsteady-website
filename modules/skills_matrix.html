Skills Matrix & Cross-Training Planner

One record that shows who can do what today, what to train next, and proof of it when someone asks.

Overview

Most sites carry the real answer to "who can do what" in one or two people's heads, usually a team leader who's been there long enough to just know — and that works only until that person is off, moves role, or leaves, at which point nobody else can say with confidence who's actually capable of covering a task. Most matrices that do get built fail for a related reason: they're built for a single reader, an auditor or a training file, and are quietly useless to everyone else.

The Skills Matrix & Cross-Training Planner is one record built for three readers at once: the team leader deciding today's rota, the individual working out what to learn next, and the auditor who needs proof someone was qualified at the moment they did the job. Ratings run on the ILUO scale — In training, Limited, Unassisted, Owner — each level defined by specific observed behaviour rather than tenure or confidence. A task only earns a row if getting it wrong could hurt someone, scrap the product, fail a downstream check, or damage the asset, or if only one person can currently do it regardless of how critical it otherwise is.

Done looks like every task and certification carrying a rated coverage, every single point of failure either on a training plan or backed by a current, priced contingency entry, and no assessment older than the standard it was rated against.

At a glance
Time

No single figure applies here, and the source material deliberately doesn't offer one: build time scales directly with team size and task count, and the guide bounds a matrix to 10–25 people and 12–20 tasks specifically so it stays a real, readable grid rather than a site-wide exercise — a 12-person, 12-task matrix and a 25-person, 20-task matrix are genuinely different amounts of work, mostly spent on assessment conversations against a defined standard, not on the spreadsheet itself. Ongoing upkeep follows the same logic: review is triggered by real events (a new task, a role change, a standard update) rather than run to a fixed weekly or monthly slot, so there's no ongoing hours-per-week figure to quote either. Any specific time estimate here would be invented, not sourced, so it's stated honestly rather than guessed.

Who's involved

Owned and maintained by the team leader; visible to the whole team, and explicitly not treated as an HR file. Assessment sign-off is role-specific: anyone rated Owner (O) on a task can assess the move from briefed to I and from I to L; moving from L to U needs the team leader or an O who didn't train that person; moving from U to O is the team leader's call alone.

What you'll need

A defined standard to rate people against for each task — this module assumes Set Standards (F2) already exists or is in progress, since ratings without a standard behind them are "opinions with a letter attached". The working file: a seven-tab workbook — Start here, Task matrix, Certifications, Roles (calculated, never typed), Assessment criteria, Sign-off record, and Contingency register.

Before you start

Confirm the team boundary first: one team leader, one rota, 10–25 people, 12–20 tasks — a matrix built for a whole site produces a grid nobody reads. If an informal skills board or partial spreadsheet already exists, check it against the working file's structure and target overlay and close what's missing rather than rebuilding from nothing.
