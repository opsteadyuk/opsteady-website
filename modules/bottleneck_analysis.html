Bottleneck Analysis Tool

The one station actually limiting your output, confirmed by a week of real numbers, before a penny gets spent on capacity.

Pricing to be announced.

Checkout isn't live yet, and pricing hasn't been set.

Overview

Every site has an opinion about what's slowing the line, usually the loudest voice or the most recent annoyance, rarely checked against data. A line's output is set by its single slowest step, not by how busy or efficient every other step looks — like a single-lane road with one slow car on it, speeding up any other car changes nothing, the queue just moves somewhere else. This tool finds that real constraint before a site signs off on new equipment, overtime or headcount to fix a capacity problem nobody can actually pin down.

The method is a week of logging throughput or cycle time at every process step, confirmed against two independent signals — WIP piling up in front of a step, and the next step standing idle waiting on it — rather than trusting whichever number looks lowest on its own. Once the constraint is named and its cost sized against the next-best-performing step, the tool deliberately doesn't jump to a capacity request: it walks the site through Exploit (getting everything the constraint can already give you for free — no idle time, no scrap, no starving it of material) and Subordinate (deliberately pacing every other station to the constraint's speed, not its own maximum) before Elevate, spending money, is ever on the table.

Done properly, a site ends the week with one named constraint, a sized cost in units or time, and a genuine first pass at the exploit/subordinate checklist — re-run monthly, since fixing one constraint reveals the next.

At a glance
Time

About 20 to 30 minutes a day to log throughput or cycle time at each step, roughly 2 to 3 hours across a representative week, plus 30 to 45 minutes at the end of the week to rank the steps and size the cost.

Who's involved

One person can run it — no new systems required. For your own site, decide: who decides which line, cell or process gets measured first; who logs throughput day to day and confirms the constraint once a week's data is in; who has to agree before a capacity request goes to Capacity Planning Model; and who gets told once the constraint is named, beyond whoever was doing the logging.

What you'll need

Output or cycle time by process step over a representative period, a week minimum. WIP counts by station are a bonus, not required.

Worked example

One week of measurement gets you one step clearly identified as the binding constraint, a sized cost in units per week, and a first attempt at getting more from that step before anyone signs off on new capital.
