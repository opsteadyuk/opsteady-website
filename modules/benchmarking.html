Benchmarking

The site knows how it actually compares, and the comparison ends in action, not a report nobody returns to.

Overview

A number in isolation feels meaningful right up until someone asks "compared to what." Without a credible comparison, a site either assumes it's doing fine because nothing looks obviously broken, or assumes it's falling behind based on a vague, unverified impression of what other sites do — both guesses dressed up as knowledge. Sites without this module tend to sit in one of two positions: no external or cross-site reference at all, or plenty of benchmark data collected and then left unused, because the definitions behind the numbers turned out to differ, or because nobody was ever actually accountable for the gap it revealed.

This module teaches how to select a small set of genuinely comparable metrics, align definitions and scope with the comparison source explicitly before comparing a single number, then turn every gap that's both genuine and significant into a named owner linked to a specific method already inside the site's existing improvement system — never a data-collection exercise ending in a slide deck. Internal comparisons, a sister site or an internal best performer, are the more reliable route wherever available, since definitions can be aligned directly through honest conversation; an external comparison against a published figure deserves proportionately more caution, since there's rarely a direct conversation available to verify what it actually measures.

Done properly, the comparison is visible somewhere the site actually looks, not buried in a one-off document, the largest genuine gaps carry named owners, and at least one gap shows a measurable improvement linked to a specific action taken because of the comparison.

At a glance
Time

Selecting metrics and aligning definitions typically takes longer than collecting the actual comparison data — budget real time for the definition conversation, since it's the step most often rushed and most likely to invalidate the whole comparison if skipped.

Who's involved

Whoever owns the operational metrics being compared, and, where the comparison is internal or cross-site, a genuine counterpart at the comparison site willing to align definitions honestly rather than just hand over a number. For your own site, decide: who chooses the metrics to benchmark; who aligns definitions with the comparison source, named specifically, since that conversation determines whether the whole exercise means something or misleads; who owns each significant gap once it's confirmed, linked to a specific existing method or module that will close it; and who decides the refresh cycle for each metric, since different metrics genuinely warrant different cycles.

What you'll need

Existing KPI definitions for the metrics being compared — the measures used have to be the same ones the site already trusts and defines consistently, since benchmarking without stable, shared definitions is just noise. Access to at least one credible comparison source: an internal best performer, a sister site, or a trustworthy external reference. The working file, E10_Benchmark_Comparison_Sheet.xlsx, one tab deliberately — metric, agreed definition, which direction is better for that metric, whether the source is internal or external, whether definitions have been confirmed matched (treating the gap as unverified until they are), this site's value, the comparison value, and the named owner and linked method for any gap that's both genuine and significant, with a "what counts as significant" threshold you set yourself, defaulting to ten per cent if left blank.

Before you start

Agree the purpose explicitly before starting: this is for learning and action, not ranking for its own sake. Confirm KPI definitions are already stable for the metrics you intend to compare, since benchmarking against a metric whose own definition is still shifting locally produces a comparison that means nothing. Work out first whether the comparison source is internal or external: internal comparisons can usually be aligned directly with a real counterpart through honest conversation, the preferred route wherever available; external comparisons often can't be verified as precisely, so treat them with proportionately more caution and state that uncertainty explicitly rather than presenting false confidence.
