Root Cause Analysis Toolkit

The real cause, verified against evidence, not just the first plausible story everyone was ready to accept.

Overview

A problem gets written up, someone offers the fastest available explanation, often "operator error," a fix goes in against that explanation, and weeks later the same problem happens again. Nobody lied — the explanation just was never actually checked against evidence before everyone agreed to act on it.

This toolkit supplies the method, matched to the problem's shape: 5 Whys for a single clear thread, the sporadic, low-occurrence, high-per-instance-impact case; a fishbone, or a fuller 8D process, for multiple tangled factors, the chronic, high-occurrence case. It's run with the people who actually do the job, not just their managers in a meeting room, and the proposed cause is verified against real evidence, records, repeat instances, direct observation, before anyone accepts it. It deliberately supplies the finding method only: A3 Problem-Solving Templates (P7.4) already verifies root cause before a countermeasure is written as part of its own structure, and this toolkit's verified cause becomes the input to that document's root-cause section, not a competing investigation.

Done properly, the verified cause is handed to whatever closes it, an A3 or the site's own structured response, with a name and a date against it — and, over time, repeat entries on the Non-Conformance Tracker for the same root cause actually drop, rather than the same problem resurfacing under a new date.

At a glance
Time

Half a day to run one RCA properly with 5 Whys, more for a genuinely tangled fishbone case.

Who's involved

Whoever actually does the job the problem happened in, not just their managers, since they're the ones who can say honestly what actually happens versus what the SOP says should happen. Whoever owns whatever will close the fix, an A3 or otherwise, since the verified cause is what they'll act on.

For your own site, decide: who runs the RCA, and who else needs to be in the room, specifically the people who do the job, not just their managers; who verifies the proposed cause against evidence before it's accepted; and who owns handing the verified cause to whatever closes it.

What you'll need

One real, current, trended problem to work on, pulled from Non-Conformance Tracker or Customer Complaint — it doesn't need to be the worst one on the list, just real. The working file, P7.5_Root_Cause_Analysis_Toolkit_Workbook.xlsx, five tabs: How to use, 5 Whys, Fishbone, Cause verification (six named checks against evidence, with a calculated stop rule that only returns "Yes" once every check reads Y with evidence behind it), and a removable Example.

Before you start

Start with the top item on the Non-Conformance Tracker, not a hypothetical or a pet issue nobody's actually raised as a priority. Tell whoever owns A3 Problem-Solving Templates, since the verified cause here becomes that document's root-cause section, not a separate parallel investigation running alongside it.

Work out first whether this is a single, clear causal thread or multiple tangled factors, matching Downtime Tracking & Pareto Pack's own occurrence-based fork: a single thread, sporadic and low-occurrence but high-impact per instance, takes 5 Whys; multiple tangled factors, chronic and high-occurrence, takes a fishbone or a fuller 8D process. Picking the wrong method for the problem's shape, 5 Whys forced onto something genuinely tangled, or a fishbone built for something that only ever needed one clean thread, is named as one of the fastest ways this toolkit gets used badly.
