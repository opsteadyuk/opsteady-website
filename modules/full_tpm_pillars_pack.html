Full TPM Pillars Pack

The right one or two additional TPM pillars, chosen from your own evidenced losses, implemented as a lightweight practice that reuses tools you already have — not a parallel eight-pillar bureaucracy.

Overview

Most sites only ever properly implement two or three of TPM's eight classic pillars, autonomous maintenance and planned maintenance among them, before either stopping there permanently or attempting all eight at once in a single ambitious programme that collapses within months. Neither outcome is a failure of intent — the full model was built for large, resourced organisations able to run eight parallel workstreams with dedicated pillar champions, a scale that doesn't transfer cleanly to most individual sites.

This module is explicitly an expansion pack, not a replacement for the core asset-care modules a site should already have running: Downtime Tracking (P5.1), PM Schedule Builder (P5.2), Autonomous Maintenance (P5.4), and ideally OEE Management (P5.5). It maps the remaining classic pillars — Quality Maintenance, Focused Improvement, Early Equipment Management, Training and Education, Safety Health and Environment, and TPM in Administration — against what's already covered, and activates only the ones that address a real, ranked loss or risk from the site's own OEE loss tree, one at a time, built wherever possible from a tool the site already owns rather than a new standalone system.

Done properly, a site can point to exactly one, or at most two, additional pillars in active use, each tied explicitly to a specific loss that's measurably improving, reviewed inside a meeting rhythm that already exists rather than a new one — and once that loss is genuinely resolved, the deliberate trigger is to retire or scale back the practice, not let it run on as ritual.

At a glance
Time

Half a day to a day to build the pillar coverage map itself, mostly a structured conversation reviewing what already exists. Implementing one chosen pillar as a minimal viable practice usually takes a few weeks, considerably less where it genuinely reuses an existing tool.

Who's involved

Whoever owns the site's OEE loss tree and existing P5 modules, since pillar selection should come directly from evidenced loss data, not preference. A cross-functional view for pillars that touch outside maintenance — quality for Quality Maintenance, HSE for Safety Health and Environment, training for Training and Education.

For your own site, decide: who decides which pillar to activate, usually the plant manager or improvement lead, working from the ranked OEE loss tree or a documented risk; who owns building the minimal viable practice for a chosen pillar, usually whoever already owns the closest existing tool it will reuse; who confirms a pillar's practice can be sustained before a second one gets activated; and who reviews whether an activated pillar is still needed once its original loss has genuinely improved.

What you'll need

A simple coverage map template and a one-page implementation sheet per activated pillar — no new software or corporate TPM materials, this module is explicitly built to avoid needing them. The working file, P5.6_Pillar_Coverage_Map.xlsx, two tabs: a Pillar coverage map (all eight pillars marked covered, activated, or deliberately not activated, with the reason and whether it addresses an evidenced loss — a pillar activated without one, or a blank entry, is flagged) and Implementation sheets, one row per activated pillar, naming the loss it addresses, the minimal practice, the existing tool it reuses, the owner, and which existing meeting it's reviewed inside.

Before you start

Confirm the core P5 modules — Downtime Tracking, PM, Autonomous Maintenance, and ideally OEE — are already stable and in genuine use before considering this expansion; a site still bedding in the basics doesn't yet have the foundation this module builds on. Make an honest assessment of real capacity for more change, not aspirational capacity, and agree explicitly that only one pillar gets piloted at a time.

Work out first whether your site is building this independently or operating under a corporate TPM or World Class Manufacturing framework imposed from above: independent sites select pillars purely from their own ranked loss data; a site under a corporate framework maps corporate-mandated pillar requirements onto the closest existing Opsteady module instead of building a parallel structure to satisfy corporate reporting alongside its own working system.
