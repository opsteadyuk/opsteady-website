Inventory & Materials Control Pack

Materials availability that doesn't quietly become the reason the plan slipped.

Pricing to be announced.

Checkout isn't live yet, and pricing hasn't been set.

Overview

When a line stops because a material ran out, the explanation written down is almost always "supply issues" — rarely false exactly, but rarely the real story either. The real story is usually that nobody had a defined minimum and maximum level, so stock only got attention once it was already gone, and the shortage arrived as a surprise rather than something anyone could see coming days or weeks in advance.

This module doesn't replace a purchasing system and doesn't try to plan every material on site — it covers the small number of critical materials whose absence actually stops production, chosen because they've genuinely caused a stoppage or delay recently, not a theoretical inventory of everything held in the stores. Each one gets a minimum level, a maximum level, and a reorder trigger sized to its lead time, so purchasing is prompted before the shelf is empty, not after. Where a material's lead time is long or variable, the trigger needs a safety stock buffer sized to the lead time's own worst realistic case, not just its average length — a supplier with a 6-week average that sometimes stretches to 9 needs a trigger that covers the 9-week case.

Done properly, the reorder trigger has actually fired a purchase order with enough lead time that a stockout genuinely doesn't happen — not a control sheet with levels filled in that still gets discovered the old way, on the floor, when a line stops.

At a glance
Time

An hour or two to list critical materials and set initial min/max levels, once real usage and lead time data exist for each.

Who's involved

Whoever holds usage data (production or planning), whoever manages supplier relationships and lead times (purchasing or procurement), and whoever owns the production plan this sheet exists to protect. For your own site, decide: who owns setting and revisiting min/max levels as usage changes; who acts when the reorder trigger fires, and how quickly; and who logs a stockout when one happens anyway, and captures why.

What you'll need

A list of materials that have actually caused a stoppage or delay in the last few months — real incidents, not a theoretical list of everything the site stocks. The working file, P4.4_Inventory_Materials_Control_Pack.xlsx, two cross-referenced tabs: a Control sheet (one row per critical material, calculating the cover the lead time needs, the reorder trigger, days of cover left, and days since last ordered — the trigger isn't typed and doesn't go stale, it moves when usage or lead time changes) and a Stockout log (material, date, cause, cost, and whether levels were revisited as a result, each entry checked against the control sheet).

Before you start

Start with the materials that have actually hurt the site recently, not an exhaustive inventory of everything held in the stores — a control sheet covering three materials that matter beats one covering 30 that mostly don't. Tell whoever places purchase orders, since the reorder trigger only works if it actually reaches them in time to act, and tell whoever owns the production plan, since materials availability is what protects it.

If an informal sense of stock levels already exists, even just in someone's head or an old spreadsheet nobody trusts, start from those thresholds and check them against real usage and lead time data rather than assuming they're still accurate, rather than building from nothing.
