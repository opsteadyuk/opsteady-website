Training Plan & Competency Sign-off

Turn every gap on your Skills Matrix into a name, a trainer and a real date — starting with the single points of failure that matter most.

Overview

Once a Skills Matrix is being used honestly, it produces red cells — that's the matrix doing its job. The problem most sites hit next isn't the matrix, it's what happens after: a page of visible gaps that closes nothing on its own, because "someone should sort that" was never attached to a person or a date.

Training Plan & Competency Sign-off is the scheduling and resourcing layer that sits on top of the matrix's gap list. It doesn't duplicate the matrix's own ILUO assessment or sign-off record — it reads the gap list, prioritises single points of failure first, and assigns each gap a trainer who already holds Owner on that task and a real calendar date.

The result is a plan, not a worry list: every red cell on the matrix ends up with a name and a date against it, and — once the scheduled date passes — a sign-off recorded back on the matrix itself.

At a glance
Time

An hour or two to pull the gap list and build the first plan, once a matrix exists. From then on, the time cost sits with the trainers delivering the sessions, not with maintaining the sheet itself.

Who's involved

Whoever owns the Skills Matrix, to supply the gap list. A named trainer for each gap, someone who already holds Owner on that specific task, not just someone senior. Whoever owns training budget or headcount approval, if the site runs one. Whoever approves time off the floor for training needs to see the prioritised list before dates are set, not after they've already slipped.

What you'll need

A completed or partial Skills Matrix — the plan is built from whatever the matrix shows today, partial or complete, rather than waiting for a finished one. A rough sense of who's currently Owner on each task, even before the matrix is finished, since that's what makes a trainer assignment real rather than a placeholder.

Before you start

Decide, for your site: who pulls the gap list from the matrix and how often; who has final say on prioritisation when two single points of failure compete for the same trainer's time; and who gets told when a scheduled date is about to slip, and how far in advance.

Start narrow in week one: pull the gap list, identify the top three single points of failure, and get real dates in the diary for those specifically before adding the rest of the sheet.
