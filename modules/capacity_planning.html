Capacity Planning Model

A capacity plan built around your actual constraint, not an assumption carried over from last year.

Pricing to be announced.

Checkout isn't live yet, and pricing hasn't been set.

Overview

Most capacity plans are built on habit — last year's numbers, adjusted up a little for this year's targets, checked against nothing in particular. They're not built around what the site can actually do, because that number usually isn't known with any precision until Bottleneck Analysis Tool (P4.1) measures it directly.

This module doesn't repeat that measurement work, and doesn't run without it — it takes the confirmed constraint's real, measured capacity and builds the plan around it: what's actually being asked of it, the sized gap between the two, and a named, recorded decision about what happens next — add capacity, flex demand, or accept the ceiling. A shortfall left sized but with no decision recorded isn't a completed plan, it's a promise the site is about to break, and headroom gets recorded too, since demand can grow into it.

Done properly, promises to customers and the production schedule are actually checked against this plan before they're made, and the plan updates automatically whenever Bottleneck Analysis Tool re-confirms or moves the constraint, rather than sitting unchanged while product mix shifts underneath it.

At a glance
Time

An hour or two, once the constraint's confirmed capacity is already in hand — most of the effort sits in Bottleneck Analysis Tool, not here.

Who's involved

Whoever owns Bottleneck Analysis Tool's confirmed constraint. Whoever owns demand or the production schedule, since that's the other half of the comparison. Whoever has authority to decide the response, since that decision usually costs money or disappoints someone.

For your own site, decide: who owns re-measuring the constraint's capacity, and how often; who has final say on the response when there's a gap; and who gets told when the plan changes, particularly anyone who's made a promise based on the old numbers.

What you'll need

Bottleneck Analysis Tool's ranked, confirmed constraint — not a guess at where the bottleneck probably is. Without that confirmed constraint, this module has nothing real to build a plan around. The working file, P4.3_Capacity_Plan_Template.xlsx: a capacity plan sheet holding the constraint capacity field (with the date last measured), the demand or schedule requirement field, a calculated gap with a plain-language readout, and a decision log tab, one row per review.

Before you start

Build this only once the constraint is genuinely confirmed, not while Bottleneck Analysis Tool is still mid-investigation — a capacity plan built around a guessed constraint is worse than no plan at all, because it looks authoritative while pointing at the wrong thing entirely. Tell whoever sets customer promises or the production schedule, since this plan is what those promises should actually be checked against going forward, and tell whoever owns Production Schedule Adherence Tracker (P4.2), since the schedule it measures adherence to should be the one this module produces.

If a capacity plan already exists for this constraint, even informally, check it against one question first: is it built around Bottleneck Analysis Tool's confirmed constraint, or around an assumption carried over from an earlier period. Most existing plans fail this test, not because they're carelessly built, but because they predate having a confirmed constraint to build around at all.
