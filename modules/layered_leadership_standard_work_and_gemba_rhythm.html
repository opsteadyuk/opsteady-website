Layered Leadership Standard Work & Gemba Rhythm

Every level of leadership actually on the floor, on a rhythm, each checking something the level below cannot see for itself.

Overview

Gemba walks usually happen when someone remembers, done by whoever's free, with no consistency in what gets checked or acted on — and senior leadership presence in particular is real in the first weeks of a new initiative and quietly gone by week 6, not because anyone decided to stop, but because nothing was ever structured to notice it fading. It's tempting to have a manager's walk just check the same things a supervisor already checks, only from someone more senior — but that wastes the one real advantage a senior walk has: the ability to see a pattern across more than one shift or area that no single supervisor, watching only their own patch, could ever spot.

This module puts a named person at each leadership level above the supervisor on their own walking rhythm — typically weekly for a shift or area manager, monthly for a plant manager — each one confirming the level below's own routine is genuinely happening, rather than re-walking the same checklist. It sits directly above Supervisor Standard Work Pack (P2.4), which needs to already be running in an area before this starts, since this module checks that routine is holding rather than replacing what it does; it is deliberately distinct from Layered Process Audit System (P2.6) too, which shares the same rotating-level mechanic but scores process and quality checkpoints against a written standard, not leadership presence and follow-through.

Done properly, a full rotation completes once with findings converted to actions, including at least one finding a lower level genuinely couldn't have surfaced on its own — and because a missed walk is tracked with the same seriousness as a bad one, a senior walk that quietly stops becomes visible fast, catching the single most common way this kind of structure fails before it's already too late to matter.

At a glance
Time

A shift or area manager's weekly walk: 30 to 45 minutes. A plant manager's monthly walk: an hour, plus time spent reading the levels below's own records first.

Who's involved

A named person at each leadership level above the supervisor. Supervisor Standard Work Pack (P2.4) needs to already be in use, since this module checks that it's holding rather than replacing it. For your own site, decide: who's the named person at each level above the supervisor; what specifically each level's walk looks for, genuinely different from the level below; and how a finding from a senior walk gets back to the right level to act on it.

What you'll need

The supervisor's own compliance register, where one exists, so the level above isn't starting from nothing when they walk.

Before you start

Confirm Supervisor Standard Work Pack is already running for the area you're starting with — if it isn't, build that first, since this module checks whether that routine is holding, it doesn't stand in for it. Start with one level, one area, before adding the next level up — a cascade built all at once, top to bottom, in a single week is harder to bed in than one confirmed to work before the next layer joins it. Tell the supervisor whose area is being walked that it's confirming the system is holding, not inspecting them personally.
