Labour Planning & Shift Pattern Model

A shift pattern and labour plan built on real coverage needs, not last year's headcount carried forward out of habit.

Overview

Most sites can't say why their current headcount is what it is. It's rarely a design, it's an accumulation — someone left and wasn't replaced, someone was added during a busy spell and never removed, a shift pattern got set years ago for reasons nobody working there now remembers — and overtime and agency spend quietly become the permanent patch for a plan that was never actually right in the first place.

This module builds the plan that should have existed from the start: role by role, shift by shift, what headcount is genuinely required — pulled from Skills Matrix coverage targets where they exist, estimated where they don't — set against what's actually there, with the gap named as a number rather than absorbed silently into overtime. A working file with a Labour plan tab and an automatically-ranked Gap summary tab tracks it by area, role and shift, with the gap signed so a positive number always means short.

The result is a labour cost problem correctly diagnosed as a planning problem: a sized, named gap with a decided fix — recruit, cross-train, or restructure the pattern itself — rather than a vague sense of being "a bit stretched," and a baseline that Overtime & Agency Spend Tracker (P8.2) can actually measure real overtime against instead of a raw, unexplained total.

At a glance
Time

Roughly half a day per area, more if Skills Matrix coverage data doesn't exist yet and requirements need estimating from scratch.

Who's involved

Whoever owns headcount decisions for the area, and whoever's watching the overtime and agency spend line, since this plan becomes their baseline the moment it exists. For your own site, decide: who owns the labour plan for each area and signs off the fix; who reviews the plan against demand each quarter, or sooner if demand shifts materially; and who's accountable for closing the gap once it's sized, not just for producing the plan.

What you'll need

Skills Matrix coverage data if it exists — useful but not required to start. The current shift pattern and current headcount. Nothing more elaborate than a spreadsheet.

Before you start

Build one area first — the highest-overtime area on site — not a site-wide rebuild; prove the gap-sizing works somewhere real before expanding it. Tell whoever runs Skills Matrix, since gaps found here should feed cross-training priorities back the other way, and whoever owns Overtime & Agency Spend Tracker (P8.2), since this plan becomes the baseline their module measures against.
