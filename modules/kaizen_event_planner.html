Kaizen Event Planner & Charter Pack

A real improvement event, chartered and resourced, not a good idea that fizzles by Wednesday.

Pricing to be announced.

Checkout isn't live yet, and pricing hasn't been set.

Overview

An improvement "event" often turns out to be a meeting with a flipchart: enthusiasm is real on day one, but there's no clear scope, no protected time, and no plan for checking whether anything actually held, so momentum dies within the week and six weeks later it's as if the event never happened. This happens because improvement effort spent without formal protection competes against the day job with nothing holding it in place, and loses every time something more urgent comes up.

This module fixes that with a charter written before anything else — a problem statement (usually Value Stream Mapping's biggest named gap), a tightly bounded scope with an explicit out-of-scope line, a named team, blocked calendar dates, a target, and a before-and-after-and-later measurement plan. The event runs against that charter, not against whatever feels urgent once the room's assembled, and the gain gets locked into a standard so it doesn't quietly erode back once the event's energy is gone. It's the sized response for a bigger, cross-functional opportunity specifically — a smaller, single-owner problem belongs with A3 Problem-Solving Templates instead, and this module explicitly says so rather than leaving the distinction implied.

Done properly, the charter's follow-up check happens at its defined point after the event, not just on the day it ends, since a gain measured once and never checked again is named as one of the easiest ways this module quietly stops proving anything.

At a glance
Time

Half a day to write the charter properly, before any event time is spent on it.

Who's involved

A real, named team, not "whoever's free that week." Someone senior enough to actually protect that team's calendar time against day-job pressure once the event starts.

For your own site, decide: who owns writing and signing off the charter before day one; who's actually on the named team, and who's protecting their calendar time against being pulled back to the day job; and who owns checking the gain again a month after the event, not just measuring it on the day.

What you'll need

A real opportunity worth a dedicated event, usually Value Stream Mapping Kit's (P7.1) biggest named gap, and genuine agreement to free people's time for it, not just verbal enthusiasm that evaporates once the calendar fills up. The working file, P7.2_Kaizen_Event_Planner_Charter_Pack.xlsx, four tabs: How to use, Charter template (with three real date fields — start, end, and the follow-up check date, sitting on the charter from the start rather than as an optional extra), Event tracker (one row per chartered event, calculating where each stands and how many days any overdue follow-up is past due), and a removable Example.

Before you start

Confirm this is genuinely a bigger, cross-functional opportunity and not a smaller, single-owner problem that belongs with A3 Problem-Solving Templates instead — chartering the wrong-sized response wastes exactly the protected time this module exists to secure. Run one chartered event at a time, not several running in parallel and competing for the same few skilled people. Tell whoever owns Value Stream Mapping Kit, since its dominant finding is what decides whether something's genuinely worth a full event, and whoever owns A3 Problem-Solving Templates, since a problem that turns out to be smaller and single-owner belongs there instead.
