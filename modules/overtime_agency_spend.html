Overtime & Agency Spend Tracker

Overtime and agency spend traced to its actual driver, not just totalled at month end.

Overview

Overtime gets a budget conversation every quarter and almost never a cause conversation: spend gets flagged, someone's told to bring it down, it dips for a month under pressure, then creeps straight back, because whatever was actually driving it was never touched. A budget line tells you the number went up; it never tells you why, so the response is always the same blunt instruction, which rarely changes anything because the underlying cause was never addressed.

This module logs every overtime or agency instance with hours, cost and a cause category — absence, capability gap, capacity shortfall, breakdown, or planning — totals and trends it monthly, ranks causes by both hours and cost (since the two can genuinely disagree, and which one matters depends on whether the real constraint is people's time or the budget line), and hands the dominant cause explicitly to whichever pillar actually owns fixing it: Skills Matrix, Capacity Planning Model, Downtime Tracking, Labour Planning, or Absenteeism & Short-Term Cover. The cross-pillar routing is the real mechanic, not the log itself — a capability gap belongs with Skills Matrix, a breakdown belongs with Downtime Tracking, and without that explicit handoff this module is just a more detailed version of the total finance already has.

Done properly, every routed cause carries a follow-up status tracked until the receiving pillar has actually picked it up, not just logged and forgotten, and the previously-dominant cause's own share of spend genuinely falls against the area's baseline over 2–3 review periods.

At a glance
Time

An hour to build the log structure and agree cause categories. After that, minutes per instance to log — the real time cost is the habit, not the setup.

Who's involved

Whoever authorises overtime and agency, since they're best placed to log the cause at the point it happens. Whoever owns each destination pillar — Skills Matrix, Capacity Planning, Downtime Tracking, Labour Planning, Absenteeism & Short-Term Cover — since a routed cause needs a real receiving process, not just a label.

For your own site, decide: who logs each overtime or agency instance, and how quickly after it happens; who reviews the monthly trend and decides which cause gets routed where; and who owns following up that a routed cause was actually picked up by the receiving pillar, not just logged and forgotten.

What you'll need

Labour Planning & Shift Pattern Model (P1.6) in some form, or at minimum an agreed baseline headcount, so "overtime" has a defined meaning — without one, every hour worked past standard could be called overtime, or none of it could, depending who's asked. The working file, P8.2_Overtime_Agency_Spend_Tracker.xlsx, three tabs: a Spend Log (one row per instance, hours, cost, a cause-category dropdown, "Routed to" filled in automatically), a Routing table (the fixed cause-to-pillar mapping the Spend Log reads from), and a Dashboard (two headline totals, an "Other" share check, a count of routed causes still open, and causes ranked worst-first both by hours and by cost).

Before you start

Confirm a labour baseline exists for the area you're starting with — if not, agree one first, however rough, since logging overtime against an undefined baseline produces a number that means nothing to anyone comparing it later. Start with one area, wherever spend is currently highest, not a site-wide rollout on day one. Tell whoever owns each of the four destination pillars, since routed causes need a real receiving process, and tell finance or leadership watching the overtime budget line, since this module reframes that conversation from a total to a cause.
