Overview

A complaint that reaches your site has usually already survived every internal check that should have caught it first, which makes it worth more attention than the average non-conformance, not less. Yet complaints are often the least systematically handled failures on site: whoever picks up the phone deals with it, apologises, maybe issues a credit, and moves on — nobody adds it to anything that shows the pattern across a quarter, so the same root cause keeps generating new complaints from new customers who have no idea it's happened before.

This module applies the same log-then-rank discipline Non-Conformance Tracker (P3.1) uses internally, just fed from outside the site instead of inside it — with one addition the internal log doesn't need: a tracked response timescale, because a complaint left unanswered for weeks costs the relationship as much as the original defect did. Closure is deliberately held to a stricter standard than "we replied": the working file distinguishes a complaint genuinely resolved from one only responded to, and flags anything past its agreed timescale whether or not a reply has gone out at all.

Done properly, the top complaint category gets handed to Root Cause Analysis by name, with the data behind it, cross-referenced against Non-Conformance Tracker wherever a cause is shared between the two — and, over successive reviews, the top complaint types actually shrink because root causes are being fixed, not just responded to faster.

At a glance
Time

An hour to agree complaint categories and a response timescale. After that, logging takes minutes per complaint, not a separate task.

Who's involved

Whoever currently receives complaints — phone, email, a portal, or all three. Whoever owns Root Cause Analysis, since the trended pattern is what gets handed to them. Whoever can commit to a response timescale on the site's behalf.

For your own site, decide: who logs a complaint — whoever receives it, or a nominated person it gets passed to; what the agreed response timescale is, and who's accountable for holding it; and who reviews the trend monthly and decides what goes to Root Cause Analysis.

What you'll need

However complaints currently arrive, even informally — phone, email, a portal. A simple, agreed list of complaint categories. The working file itself: P3.2_Customer_Complaint_Log_and_Trend.xlsx, two tabs — a Complaint log and a calculated Trend view — cross-referenced to Non-Conformance Tracker's own log where a root cause is shared between the two.

Before you start

Start logging every complaint from today, however it arrives, before trying to unify every channel into one intake system — a complaint logged inconsistently from three different channels still beats a beautifully designed single channel that isn't live yet. Tell everyone who might receive a complaint before the first one arrives, so it isn't logged inconsistently just because whoever took the call didn't know the system existed, and tell whoever owns Root Cause Analysis, since the monthly handoff depends on them expecting it.

If some version of a complaint record already exists, even a shared inbox or a folder of resolved emails, pull every existing record into one place first, even where the detail is inconsistent — a rough, complete picture beats a precise, partial one.
