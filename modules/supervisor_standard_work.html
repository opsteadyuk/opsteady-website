Supervisor Standard Work Pack

A fixed round through the shift, covering safety, quality, people and delivery, so what gets checked doesn't depend on what's loudest today.

Overview

Supervisors are routinely reactive not because they lack judgement, but because nothing structures their day for them. Whatever shouts loudest wins every time, and the things that would have prevented the next fire — a supervision gap, an unflagged hold, a single point of failure going uncovered — don't get checked until they've already become one.

This module gives a supervisor a fixed round: what gets checked, when, in what order, every shift, regardless of what else is happening — a route through the shift the supervisor actually walks, not a checklist completed at the end of the day from memory. It's built from a shadowed real shift, not a template or memory, across three fixed time-blocks (shift start, mid-shift, pre-handover) plus a continuous safety strip, checking four categories: Safety, Quality, People, Delivery. Cost is deliberately left out — that belongs to the KPI cascade and performance board, not a single supervisor's shift-by-shift round.

Done well, the round is followed without prompting for two weeks running, with genuine completion in the register rather than everything ticked at the same time each day — and a shift manager reading the compliance register alone, without asking the supervisor anything, can tell whether today's round actually happened or was reconstructed afterwards.

At a glance
Time

Roughly one full shift to shadow, a few hours to turn the log into a working sheet. Once running: 10 to 15 minutes a shift for the supervisor to walk the round itself, on top of the work they're already doing, not instead of it.

Who's involved

Owned by the supervisor the round is built for, agreed with whoever owns rollout across the rotation (a shift manager or team leader). The shadow shift itself should be run by that manager, not the supervisor watching themselves, since people are reliably bad at seeing their own reactive patterns from the inside.

For your own site, decide: who decides the checkpoint list for this shift pattern; who can change it once it's running; who gets told when it changes; and who has to agree before a checkpoint is removed, especially a safety one.

What you'll need

A Skills Matrix for this team makes the People column genuinely load-bearing, since it's where real single points of failure and in-progress transitions come from — not a blocker to start, since the three critical-risk questions alone are enough to build a working round, but a reason to revisit the People checkpoints once a matrix exists, even a partial one. The working file itself: a printable standard work sheet, a compliance register, and a dashboard — three tabs, not a single sheet. Note the Dashboard's Round maturity calculation needs a modern Excel (Microsoft 365 or Excel 2021+); everything else in the workbook works on any Excel version.

Before you start

Don't design this from memory or a template — the first real action is shadowing a real shift and logging what actually happens against what should happen, on an ordinary shift, not one chosen because it's expected to be quiet. Build the sheet in the same week as the shadow shift, while it's still fresh, and trial it with the one supervisor shadowed before it goes anywhere near the wider rotation.

If some version of this already exists informally — a habit, a half-remembered checklist, a laminated sheet nobody's updated in a year — shadow anyway: what a supervisor says they do and what they actually do under pressure are often two different things. Check the existing version against the four categories and the real gaps, and close what's missing rather than starting from nothing.
