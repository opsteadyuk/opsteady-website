Idea & Suggestion System

Ideas from anyone, actually answered, not swallowed into a box nobody opens.

Overview

Most sites have run a suggestion scheme at some point, and most of those schemes are quietly dead — not cancelled, just stopped, a box nobody empties or a form nobody reads, because at some point ideas went in and nothing visible ever came back out. Once that happens once too often, people stop submitting, not out of apathy but because they learned, correctly, that it doesn't do anything.

This module rebuilds the scheme around one mechanic: a genuinely zero-friction way to submit an idea, a real, stated commitment to respond to every single one within a set number of working days — including the ones the answer is no to — and every idea logged with its status visible to everyone, not locked in a manager's inbox. A declined idea, handled honestly with a real written reason and traced rather than ignored, does more for trust in the scheme than a second accepted idea would, since submitters are watching how "no" gets handled at least as closely as how "yes" does.

It depends directly on Culture (F3): the mechanic of closing the loop on every idea, including declines, is the concrete version of Culture's four-step response applied specifically to suggestions, and without that underlying psychological safety, submissions tend to drift toward complaints or score-settling instead of real ideas. Done well, submission volume holds or grows over time — the clearest sign people believe the scheme actually works — rather than quietly tailing off the way the last one did.

At a glance
Time

An afternoon to set up the submission method and the tracker. The real, ongoing time cost is responding within the committed timescale, every cycle.

Who's involved

Someone who owns closing the loop on every idea, checks the response deadline, and actually writes the decision down, even when the answer is no. For your own site, decide: who owns responding to every idea within the committed timescale, including declines; what the actual submission method is, and whether it's genuinely zero-friction for the person using it; and where status lives, and whether everyone can actually see it without asking.

What you'll need

A genuinely zero-friction submission method — a physical box, a shared form, a message to a supervisor, whatever removes the most friction on your site — and a visible tracker or board everyone can actually see, not a spreadsheet only one person opens. The working file, P7.3_Idea_Suggestion_Tracker_Template.xlsx: an Idea tracker tab (one row per idea, with an Overdue column that flags itself automatically once the response due date passes without a closed decision) and a visible status board mirroring the tracker's status column for anyone to check without opening a spreadsheet.

Before you start

If this is a relaunch, respond to every idea currently sitting unanswered, even old ones, before asking for a single new submission — trust has to be rebuilt before it can be spent, and asking for fresh ideas while old ones sit unanswered repeats the exact failure that killed the scheme the first time. Tell whoever's been asked for ideas before and never heard back; their first experience of the relaunched scheme should be a real answer to what they already submitted. Set the submission method and a real, stated response timescale before launching, and make both genuinely visible.
