Non-Conformance Tracker & Trend Pack

Every non-conformance logged and trended, not just counted in a monthly report after the fact.

Overview

A single non-conformance always looks like a one-off — whoever catches it deals with it and moves on, because dealing with the part in front of them is the actual job. Nobody's job, most of the time, is to notice that the same one-off has happened 11 times this month, scattered across 3 shifts, three inspectors' private notebooks and one supervisor's memory. When right-first-time drops and someone asks why, the honest answer is that nobody can say, because no single record ever held all of it in one place.

This module fixes that with the simplest possible structure: log every non-conformance as it happens, even roughly categorised, and rank the log monthly by both frequency and cost, since the two don't always point at the same defect. The working file is one connected workbook — an NC log, an editable Categories tab, a calculated Pareto tab (with a "minimum entries before this feels representative" setting, defaulting to 20), and a six-month Trend view — so the pattern that was invisible scattered across three notebooks becomes obvious the moment it's all in one column.

What separates this from counting defects is that the cost field means the same thing every time. The basis is what it cost you to get the part to the point it failed: raw material plus the processing already put into it, not what the customer would have paid for it. That basis needs one adjustment per disposition, because four of the five do not behave like scrap — rework counts the labour and machine time to put it right, but not the material already in the part, because the part is not lost; use as-is counts nothing unless a concession was given; and a return the supplier does not credit is a scrap entry, not a return. Forty pounds of rework labour and forty pounds of scrapped material are both forty pounds actually gone, so the Pareto can add across dispositions without lying about anything.

On a multi-line site it is one workbook per line, not one log with a line column. Take a copy of the supplied file for each line and give each copy an owner: whoever runs that line's daily board. The reason is ownership rather than tidiness — a ranking nobody owns at a daily board is a report, not a tool. Four lines, four files, four owners, and no tool change. Rolling up to a site view is the exception, and it carries a condition: the lines have to be genuinely identical. If they are not, the rolled-up Pareto sums codes that do not mean the same thing on each line, and the site-wide top bar is an artefact of the addition rather than a fact about the site.

Three modules connect to this one, and each one asks you to do something. Poka-Yoke & Andon System (P3.3) is downstream and the one relationship that gates: you cannot mistake-proof a defect you have not yet ranked, so P3.3 needs one recurring defect already ranked from this tracker, and a Pareto that has reached Established, before it can start. Root Cause Analysis Toolkit (P7.5) is downstream and the destination — this module ranks, P7.5 investigates, and a tracker with no handoff is a tidy list of problems nobody is working on. Waste & Cost-of-Quality Calculator (P8.1) is the source of the cost basis this module ranks on: the rule for what goes in the cost field, and the per-disposition adjustments, come from P8.1's cost-of-quality categories.

At a glance
Time

An hour to agree defect categories and set the log up. After that, logging itself takes seconds per entry, not a separate task.

Who's involved

Whoever's currently catching non-conformances day to day — inspectors, operators, whoever's actually there when something's found — and whoever owns Root Cause Analysis, since they're the destination for whatever this log surfaces. For your own site, decide: who logs a non-conformance, whoever finds it or a nominated person per shift; who reviews the Pareto monthly and decides what goes to RCA; and who can change the defect category list once it's agreed, and how often. On a multi-line site, that owner is per line: whoever runs that line's daily board.

What you'll need

The NC Log, Pareto & Trend workbook (Excel), supplied with this module. A working or emerging OTIF & Right-First-Time Dashboard, or at minimum a live concern that RFT is dropping — a finished dashboard isn't required to start. A simple, agreed list of defect categories, not perfect, agreed, which goes straight onto the workbook's own Categories tab.

Before you start

Start logging today, even with rough categories, rather than waiting for a perfect taxonomy that never quite gets finished — three months of roughly categorised data starting today is worth more than three months of properly categorised data starting next quarter. Tell everyone who'll be logging, so the first entry isn't a surprise, and tell whoever owns Root Cause Analysis, since the monthly handoff depends on them expecting it.

If some version of a log already exists, scattered across notebooks or individual spreadsheets, pull every existing record into one place first, even where categories don't match — reconciling categories after consolidation is easier than standardising before anyone's agreed to log centrally at all.

Agree what goes in the cost field before the first entry, not after the first argument about it. The basis and its five per-disposition rules are set out in the guide, and the whole cost-versus-frequency comparison rests on that number meaning the same thing every time.
