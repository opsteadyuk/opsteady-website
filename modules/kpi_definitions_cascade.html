KPI Definitions & Cascade Guide

One agreed definition per number, so the argument moves from whose number is right to what to do about it.

£349Buy now
Overview

A KPI Blueprint tree tells you what feeds what. It doesn't tell you what any box on it actually means, and that gap has a specific, predictable shape: the same KPI name gets calculated three different ways by three different people, and every one of them is defensible on its own terms — Quality excludes rework from first-pass yield because it's a separate process in their view, Operations includes it because from the floor it's all one pass, Finance pulls a rolling weekly average from a different system entirely.

This tool fixes that with a definitions register: one row per measure, holding the exact formula, the data source, the owner, and the cascade level it sits at on the tree. Once a number has all four, "what does that actually mean" stops being a live question every time it's raised. Building it starts with the numbers people already argue about — bringing everyone currently reporting a version into the same room, comparing formulas and sources openly, and agreeing one version as a group, rather than working through every box on the tree in order.

Once a definition is registered and cited, disputes over what a number means fall away over time; the adoption ladder's top stage is meetings opening on what a number means for the decision at hand, not on re-establishing whose version is correct.

At a glance
Time

Half a day for the first handful of genuinely disputed numbers. Less once the pattern's established, since most measures don't have three competing versions — only the loudest few do.

Who's involved

Everyone currently reporting a version of the disputed number, brought into the room together rather than consulted separately; whoever owns the measure on the Blueprint tree; and whoever holds final sign-off on the register overall. For your own site, this tool asks you to name: who proposes a definition (the measure's owner, or whoever raises the dispute), who has final sign-off before a definition is agreed and registered, and who gets told when an existing definition changes, and how far in advance.

What you'll need

A built or partial KPI Blueprint tree (F1.1) — but this isn't a hard prerequisite: without one, start with the site's single most argued-about number instead of waiting for a tree that may not exist yet. Start with the numbers people already argue about, not an exhaustive pass through every measure on the tree — a register built by working through every box in tree order takes weeks and covers plenty of numbers nobody's ever disputed.

Before you start

Tell anyone whose reporting currently relies on a version of the number that's about to be superseded — a definition that changes underneath someone without warning reads as their number being declared wrong, not as a site-wide agreement. Decide, for your own site: who proposes a definition, who has final sign-off, and who gets told when a definition changes.
