Waste & Cost-of-Quality Calculator

Two frameworks kept honest, not forced together — the eight lean wastes and the four cost-of-quality categories, meeting at exactly one point, so neither gets misrepresented.

Overview

Two real costs most sites feel but rarely see written down in pounds: waste, the lean version (eight named categories, the same ones taught by 8 Wastes (TIMWOODS) Identification Toolkit), and cost of quality, the accounting version (four categories — prevention, appraisal, internal failure, external failure — credited to Juran and Feigenbaum's PAF model). Both measure real money leaving the business, but from different vantage points, and most sites have never actually reconciled the two. That produces a specific, recurring mistake: someone tries to make one tidy number out of both frameworks, and ends up with a figure that satisfies neither a lean audience nor a finance audience, because it quietly misrepresents whichever framework it borrowed less carefully from.

The two frameworks are related, not the same thing — defects waste and quality failure cost are the same event seen from two angles, but the other seven wastes are real money without being a "quality" cost in the strict sense. This tool keeps them as two logs, not one forced structure, cross-referencing a row only where a Defects waste entry and a Failure cost entry are genuinely the same event, so the two totals can be read side by side without ever being added together. It also names what it deliberately won't capture: hidden costs like firefighting hours, delayed launches, or damaged customer trust, documented as running several times higher than the visible tracked figure — recorded as a known gap on the Definitions tab rather than guessed at, so the total reads as a floor, not a ceiling.

Done properly, the top cost or waste category gets handed to Root Cause Analysis Toolkit by name once it's stopped moving across three review periods, not left sitting at the top of the Dashboard as an observation nobody owns.

At a glance
Time

Half a day to set your definitions, then logging as events happen, a few minutes each time.

Who's involved

Whoever owns quality costs and whoever owns waste identification — often the same person on an SME site. For your own site, decide: who decides the scope (narrow COPQ or full COQ) stays as it currently sits; who's allowed to propose a change to the scope or cost bases; who gets told when either changes; and who needs to agree first if a cost-of-quality figure already gets reported to finance or a board.

What you'll need

The workbook, and a rough sense of your labour and materials cost basis so entries are consistent. The working file itself: four tabs — Definitions, Cost of Quality Log, Waste Log, Dashboard — with both log tabs shipping one worked example row, explicitly marked to delete before you start.

Before you start

Define your cost-of-quality scope — narrow COPQ (failure costs only) or full COQ (prevention, appraisal, internal and external failure) — before logging a single cost; both are legitimate, what breaks a number is not saying which one you mean. If you're auditing a figure finance already tracks, check what's actually in it first, since most finance-tracked "cost of quality" figures are narrower than they sound, often scrap value alone. If a cost-of-quality figure already gets reported upward, tell whoever receives that report before you touch the scope definition.

If time is genuinely short, run the Cost of Quality Log alone first — it's the sharper financial argument and the one most likely to get budget released — and add the Waste Log once that habit holds.
