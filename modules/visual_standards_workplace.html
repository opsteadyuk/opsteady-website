Visual Standards & Workplace Organisation Guide

Every station with a defined place for everything, and a standard you can see, not one you have to ask about.

£179Buy now
Overview

Without a physical, visible standard at the point of use, "normal" lives in people's heads, and everyone's head holds a slightly different version. A new starter invents their own layout. A returning operator remembers it differently than the person who covered their shift. Nobody's wrong exactly, there was just never anything to be right against — an inconsistency that isn't anyone's fault individually, because normal was never actually defined anywhere a person could check it.

This module fixes that by making the standard physical: photograph the current state honestly, agree the standard state (item, location, quantity) with whoever actually works the station, mark it physically with tape, labels or a shadow board — cheap and easy to change, not paint or engraving — then post a one-point lesson at the point of use, roughly 80% picture to 20% words. It applies Visual Management Foundations' (F4) same stranger-test discipline comprehensively, to every station, rather than to F4's deliberately narrow handful of critical points.

Done properly, a station reaches Control level or above on the four-level control ladder (Indicator, Signal, Control, Guarantee) — the layout itself shapes what a person can do, not just a sign telling the rule — and it's this module's own register status that tells 5S Audit Pack (P6.1) whether there's a real standard yet to audit against: you can't meaningfully score compliance to a standard that was never actually set down.

At a glance
Time

A phone camera, labels or tape, half a day per station type. No budget required beyond consumables.

Who's involved

Whoever actually works the station, since the standard needs to reflect what genuinely works, not an outsider's guess. Whoever owns the area, since they'll need to hold the standard once it's set.

For your own site, decide: who owns building the standard for each station, together with whoever works it; who checks that a posted standard is being held, and on what rhythm; and who decides when a station is ready to count as done, at Control level or above.

What you'll need

The Visual Standards Register (Excel), supplied with this module. A phone camera for before-and-after photos. Labels, tape or a shadow board for marking the physical standard. Nothing more elaborate than that to start.

Before you start

Build one area at a time, not a site-wide sweep — one station built properly before starting the next, since a dozen stations photographed and none of them actually labelled is worse than three done right. Tell whoever runs 5S Audit Pack, if it's already in use, since this module is what that audit scores against, and tell whoever's onboarding new starters, since a well-built standard is one of the fastest ways to get someone productive without constant supervision.

Check first whether any informal standard already exists for the station, even just habit or a verbal handover: if from nothing, define the standard directly with whoever will work it; if replacing an informal arrangement, photograph the messy reality first, including its inconsistencies, before defining what the standard should actually be — don't skip straight to the tidy version.
