Going Digital

Know what's actually worth moving off paper, and what works better staying there.

Overview

Sites go wrong in this area in two opposite directions. Stuck entirely on paper, they miss real gains a screen would genuinely deliver — trend history nobody can hold in their head, visibility for people who aren't physically on site. Rushed onto a screen too early, they lose what paper was quietly doing for free — instant, walk-by visibility, no login, no battery, no app to open. Both directions get decided by momentum or appearance rather than evidence: a board digitised because it looked modern, or left on paper because moving it felt like effort, neither ever actually tested.

This module applies a simple test to each tracker or board individually, not as a blanket policy: does someone genuinely need it remotely, does the data need real calculation or trending that's a burden by hand, is the physical version itself failing. Digitise only where the test clears the bar, with at least one clear, weighty yes — leave everything else exactly where it is. An external mandate to digitise doesn't switch the test off; it changes what the test is for, informing a conversation with whoever's mandating it rather than settling the question outright. It exists specifically to protect Visual Management Foundations' (F4) premise — that a physical, visible standard works because it costs nothing to check — from being quietly undermined by digitisation decided on appearance.

Done properly, every tracker has a stated, deliberate reason for being paper or digital, not an assumption, and new digitisation requests get tested before being agreed to, not rubber-stamped after.

At a glance
Time

Half a day to list what's currently tracked on paper or whiteboard and run the test against each.

Who's involved

Whoever owns each tracker or board, since they know whether the remote-access need or the calculation burden is genuinely real. Whoever's pushing for digitisation — corporate, a customer, or an internal instinct to look modern — since that pressure needs weighing against the test, not deferred to automatically.

For your own site, decide: who owns applying the test to each tracker or board, and who has final sign-off on the outcome; who represents the case for digitising where there's genuine pressure to; and who reviews the decision list periodically, since a right call today can become the wrong one later as volume or audience changes.

What you'll need

A list of what's currently tracked on paper or whiteboard — nothing more elaborate is needed to start. The working file, E3_Going_Digital_Decision_Pack.xlsx, two tabs: a Decision checklist (one row per tracker or board, the three test questions answered, whether a real external mandate exists, a calculated test result and recommendation, the decision actually made, and the reason — where the decision goes against the test, the sheet says so rather than quietly agreeing) and a Running list (the persistent record of every judgement made, checked back against the checklist so a tracker that never went through the test is named rather than sitting there looking settled).

Before you start

Run the test first against whichever board everyone already assumes "should" be digital, since that's usually where the biggest gap between assumption and evidence sits.

Work out first whether there's a real external mandate — corporate or customer — to digitise this specific tracker: if yes, apply the test anyway and use the result to have an informed conversation with whoever's mandating it, rather than simply complying or refusing; if no, apply the test standalone — "looks modern" isn't one of the three questions, and shouldn't quietly become a fourth one.
