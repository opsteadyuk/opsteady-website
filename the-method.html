The Method

Four stages. One loop. Re-entered, not finished.

Operations aren't good or bad — they're somewhere. The Opsteady Method is how you read where, and what to do next in an order that actually holds. Nothing here is new. What's usually missing is the order.

The four stages

01 · Observe & Learn

Understand before you change

Watch how the work actually happens, not how the procedure says it happens. The Health Check lives here — it's the entry point into everything else.

02 · Stabilise

Stop the firefighting

Make today repeatable before you try to make it better. A standard everyone actually follows, not one that just looks good in the folder.

03 · Improve

Improve from a base that holds

Once today is predictable, raise it. Improvement built on a moving target drifts back the moment attention moves elsewhere.

04 · Sustain

Make it stick without heroics

No one person carrying it, no burst of energy that fades. Then you re-enter at Observe & Learn — an operation doesn't graduate, it keeps cycling with the bar a little higher.

Why the order matters

None of the individual ideas here are ours — standard work, visual management, tackling your biggest constraint first, these are documented territory, credited where we use them. What most operations are missing isn't a technique. It's the order, and knowing what one move quietly undoes another.

  • Fix the standard before you improve it

    Improving a standard nobody actually follows just moves the gap somewhere else. Get the floor and the paperwork saying the same thing first — that's Set Standards, and it's why it's a Foundation, not a pillar you get to later.

  • Trust the signal before you act on it

    A KPI built on numbers nobody checks is a guess with a decimal point. See what's really happening before you build the measurement architecture, and build that before you chase a target with it.

  • Trust before audits

    Check compliance before people trust the process, and you get compliance theatre — problems don't stop, they just go quiet. Culture isn't a soft add-on here; it's the medium every other tool runs through.

  • Three moves, not twelve

    Everything at once beats nothing — but it also beats ownership. Every Health Check report gives three moves, in order, not a list of everything that's wrong. Three done properly outlasts twelve half-started.

What "a step-by-step Monday" actually means

If you've already read a Health Check report, you've seen this phrase and never had it explained — that gap gets closed here.

Every move your report recommends comes with a Monday: the exact first action, broken into steps you could start this coming Monday morning. Not a project plan. Not a course to book. Not "consider reviewing your approach to..." — the actual first thing you do, and the one after that.

Most Mondays are things you start today, on the floor, with what you've already got. Once in a while, the honest first move is watching for a week before you touch anything — and when that's true, we say so plainly, rather than dressing up "wait and see" as a bigger fix than it is.

A worked example, for a site whose standards live mostly in people's heads:

  1. Pick the one process where losing the person who knows it would hurt most.
  2. Get that person to walk it with you, exactly as they actually do it — not how the old sheet says.
  3. Write it down in the order they do it, in their words.
  4. Have a second person run it from the write-up alone, and note where they got stuck.
  5. Fix those gaps. That's your first standard — one, done properly, not twelve done badly.
