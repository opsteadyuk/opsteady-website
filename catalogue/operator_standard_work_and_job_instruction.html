Operator Standard Work & Job Instruction

Most critical tasks on a manufacturing floor are learned the same way: watch someone experienced do it a few times, then have a go yourself while they check in occasionally. It feels efficient, and for a while it is, but it quietly produces as many versions of "the right way" as there are experienced people teaching it. Each one has their own shortcuts, their own emphasis, their own unspoken reasons for doing a step a particular way that never gets said out loud because it's obvious to them.

What it takes

Setup timecapturing one task properly, from watching the work to a trial-taught instruction, typically takes half a day to a day, spread across a few sessions rather than one long sitting, since the person doing the capturing needs to watch the work at a normal pace more than once.
Who's involvedwhoever's capturing the instruction, the person who currently does the task best, and one other person willing to be trial-taught from the draft before it's finalised.
Materialsthe job instruction template, and Set Standards (F2)'s register if one already exists, so the finished instruction has somewhere to be logged rather than living as a loose document.

Common questions

What if the best performer doesn't want their method written down?
This usually comes from the shortcut being framed as an assessment rather than a request to capture expertise. Lead with why: their method is what's actually going to train the next person, and they're the one confirming it's right, not the one being judged. See section 16.
How detailed should a key point actually be?
Specific and teachable, not a full procedure. A key point names exactly what a new person wouldn't know to do without being told, and the reason names what goes wrong if they don't. If a key point takes more than a sentence or two to write, it's often really two key points, or it's drifting into procedure territory. See Pro section 14.
What if two experienced operators do the task differently and both insist they're right?
Watch both, and check whether the difference is a genuine key point (something that affects the outcome) or just habit. If there's a safe way to test which method actually produces a better or more consistent result, do that together rather than picking a side. If it turns out both methods genuinely work, that's a real multi-variant task, see Pro section 13.
Does this replace Set Standards (F2)?
No. Set Standards is where the discipline of writing down the right way and keeping it matched to practice lives. This module takes a standard, or the raw material for one, and turns it into the specific three-column format a task gets trained from. See section 10.
What if the task is only a few seconds long, on a highly automated line?
A written three-column document is usually the wrong format at that pace. See Pro section 13 for the visual variant: a single annotated photograph or diagram at the workstation, with the reasons captured verbally during training rather than written out in full.
