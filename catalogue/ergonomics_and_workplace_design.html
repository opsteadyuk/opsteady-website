Common questions

What if we can't find an obvious physical fix?
Rank the finding anyway and hold it rather than dropping it. Not every problem has an immediate low-cost fix, some genuinely need a larger capital change. Log it, make whatever partial interim improvement is available now, and route the larger request through your site's normal capital process rather than letting the finding sit unrecorded. See Pro section 17, fork 5.
How do we know if something needs a formal occupational health or DSE assessment instead of a shop-floor fix?
The signal is whether you're dealing with an observed design fault (a reach, an awkward posture, a repeated motion) or a reported symptom (pain, numbness, a specific physical complaint). A design fault is this module's territory. A reported symptom needs a direct, prompt referral to whoever owns occupational health on your site, or a qualified professional where none exists internally. See section 4's minimum decision fork and Pro section 13.
What if the person doing the job resists the change we've made?
Check first whether the fix genuinely matches how the work's actually done, a change designed without watching the real work often misses something and gets reasonably resisted. If it does match, ask directly what's making it hard to adopt, sometimes a genuinely good fix still needs a short adjustment period. See section 9's second failure.
Does this replace a formal risk assessment?
No. This module is a practical observation and improvement method for the physical design of a task. Where your site has statutory risk assessment requirements, this module supports that work by supplying real, current observation, it doesn't replace the formal process itself.
What if the best fix would need investment we don't have right now?
Make whatever partial change is genuinely available now, even if it's not the ideal fix, rather than waiting for full budget approval before doing anything. See Pro section 14: an available partial fix now beats a perfect fix that never gets funded.
