Common questions

What actually counts as specific versus generic?
Generic names the group and stops there: "well done team." Specific names the person, the exact thing they did, and why it mattered: "Amir flagged a developing quality issue before it left the cell, which likely saved a downstream rework." If you can't tell from reading the entry later exactly what happened, it's generic. See section 3.
What if one leader is naturally better at this than the others?
That's common, and it's a reason to use them as the visible example, not a reason to leave the others to catch up on their own. See section 15: this module treats specific recognition as a taught skill, not a personality trait, and the prompt card exists to close that gap deliberately.
Does this replace our existing rewards or bonus scheme?
No, and it shouldn't be positioned as one. A bonus scheme rewards results against a target. This module notices specific behaviours and moments, many of which a formal scheme structurally can't see, in the flow of everyday work. Keep the two explicitly separate. See Pro section 13.
What if recognising someone repeatedly for the same catch is actually masking a process problem?
That's worth noticing and acting on. Keep recognising the individual catch, since it's still genuinely good work, but route the underlying recurring issue to Root Cause Analysis or the relevant pillar module. See section 9 and Pro section 17, fork 4.
Do we need Culture (F3) in place before starting this?
Ideally yes, at least in some working form. Recognition, especially given publicly, only lands as recognition when people trust the site's response to problems is fair. Without that, being singled out can feel like exposure. If Culture isn't yet established, start with private, one-to-one recognition rather than a visible board. See section 4's minimum decision fork.
