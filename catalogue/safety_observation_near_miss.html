Common questions

Why does Culture (F3.1) have to come first?
Because reporting behaviour is gated by trust, not by how good the reporting method is. The moment someone believes raising a near miss will get them blamed rather than heard, reporting dries up, however frictionless the card or form is. This module doesn't create that trust, it depends on it already being there. If Culture hasn't taken hold on your site, that's the actual first step, not this module. See guide section 2 and section 13.
Isn't a rising near-miss count a bad sign?
The opposite, on a site with genuine psychological safety. A large base of near misses sits beneath every serious injury, so a rising count means more of that prevention capacity is being captured, not that the site's getting more dangerous. Watch the injury trend, not the report count, for whether things are actually improving. See guide section 8 and section 13.
How is this different from HSE System (E9.1)?
HSE owns the formal compliance record. This module owns the daily behavioural layer, walking the floor and spotting things before they need a formal investigation. The line between them is a threshold your site sets for what escalates into HSE's register. Anything below that threshold stays in this lightweight log. See guide section 10 and section 18, fork 3.
What if we haven't agreed an escalation threshold with whoever owns HSE System yet?
Build this module anyway. Agree a working threshold yourself in the meantime, using the guide's own starting example if you don't have a better one, and revisit it once that conversation with HSE's owner has actually happened. See guide section 4.
Do we need a fancy reporting tool to start?
No. A card, a laminated tag, a simple form or just telling a supervisor who logs it the same day. Whatever removes the most friction for the person reporting is the right answer, not whatever looks most sophisticated. See guide section 5.
