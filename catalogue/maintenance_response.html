Common questions

How is this different from PM Schedule Builder?
PM Schedule Builder fixes breakdowns caused by maintenance being skipped or done late. This module fixes breakdowns where the response after the fact is the problem, unclear who to call, parts not on hand, no defined escalation point. Both branch off the same Downtime Tracking Pareto, and the Pareto tells you which one your site actually needs first. See guide section 10.
How do we know if response time, rather than breakdown frequency, is our real issue?
Check Downtime Tracking's data directly. If the Pareto is dominated by how long it takes to resolve breakdowns once they happen, rather than how often they happen, this is the right branch. If it's the reverse, PM Schedule Builder or a root-cause investigation into breakdown frequency itself is the better starting point. See guide section 4.
Do we need priority tiers, or is one response chain enough?
Depends on whether your breakdowns are genuinely similar in severity. If a safety-critical line stop and something that can wait until shift end get treated the same way today, tiering usually helps: each priority level gets its own target time and escalation trigger, so a serious issue doesn't wait behind a minor one and a minor one doesn't trigger the same urgency as a serious one. If your breakdowns are mostly similar in how urgent they are, one fixed chain is simpler and just as effective, adding tiers you don't need just adds a classification step nobody trusts. See guide section 5, fork 1, and section 14.
What if nobody wants to be the one who escalates?
That's the single most common way an escalation trigger fails even after it's been defined. Reframe escalation as the structure working exactly as intended, not as an admission that the first response failed. The trigger exists precisely so nobody has to make that judgement call alone under pressure. See guide section 9 and section 17 (Pro).
Do we need to map every asset's response path, or just the worst one?
Start with the single asset causing the worst response-time pain. A structure that's real and trusted for one asset beats one written for every asset with nobody actually briefed on it. See guide section 7.
