Common questions

Does this replace our purchasing system?
No. This module doesn't place orders, manage suppliers or run procurement. It covers a small number of critical materials whose absence stops production, with a visible trigger that tells purchasing when to act. Purchasing still does the purchasing. See the guide's opening distinction.
How do we pick which materials to cover first?
Start with materials that have actually caused a stoppage or delay recently, real incidents, not a theoretical list of everything the site stocks. Three materials with working reorder triggers beats 30 tracked loosely. See guide section 4.
What if stock still runs out even with a trigger set?
Log it in the stockout log with cause, immediately. A stockout against a material that's supposedly under control is direct evidence the min/max levels are wrong, not proof that supply is simply unreliable. That log entry is exactly the data that fixes the levels going forward. See guide section 5 and section 8.
How is min/max level different from just watching stock and reordering when it looks low?
A defined minimum with a trigger means the reorder happens the moment stock crosses that point, not whenever someone happens to notice and think it looks low. "Looks low" is a judgement call made too late, after the shortage is already close. A trigger is a number, checked automatically, that fires with enough lead time to actually act. See guide section 2.
Our lead times vary a lot between suppliers. Does this module handle that?
Yes, as a Pro variant. Multi-supplier materials get lead time and reliability tracked per supplier rather than a single blended figure, with the currently live supplier made explicit for the reorder trigger to reference. See guide section 14 (Pro) or section 11 (Essentials).
