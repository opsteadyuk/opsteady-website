HSE System

**Why this module exists.** Opsteady isn't a health and safety consultancy, and this module doesn't try to define or run your HSE programme for you. Some sites already have a dedicated HSE team or manager doing exactly that, in more depth than a generic catalogue module ever could. What this exists for is narrower, and easier to miss: the rest of this catalogue is about change, a new layout, a rebalanced line, an updated SOP, a redesigned process. Change like that can carry HSE consequences that nobody thinks to check, precisely because whoever's making the change is thinking about output, flow, or quality, not compliance. A minor SOP update might need its underlying risk assessment reviewed. A layout change might affect fire escape routes or the manual handling distances an operator now covers. This module's job is to make sure that question gets asked, not to become the thing that has to answer every HSE question itself, and not to become a reason improvement work stalls waiting for a sign-off it may not even need. Common questions

Is this guide legal advice? Can we rely on it if we get audited?
No, and don't. This module explains what obligations generally look like and how to track them, it isn't legal advice, and it doesn't replace a competent health and safety adviser. The specific categories, intervals, and thresholds referenced (RIDDOR's categories, LOLER's examination intervals, environmental permit and trade effluent conditions) are set and revised by the relevant regulator, HSE, the Environment Agency, your water company, your local authority, not by Opsteady. Confirm current requirements at the source before relying on anything here for a live compliance decision. See guide section 4.
Why does Opsteady, a continuous improvement catalogue, even have an HSE module? Isn't that a specialist area?
It is, and this module doesn't try to be a specialist in it. What it exists for is narrower: the rest of this catalogue is about change, layouts, methods, line balance, equipment. Change like that can carry HSE consequences nobody thinks to check, because whoever's making it is thinking about output or flow, not compliance. A minor SOP update might need its risk assessment reviewed. A layout change might affect fire escapes or manual handling. This module's job is to make sure that question gets asked, and to hold the compliance record itself, not to run your HSE programme for you. See guide section 2.
We already have a dedicated HSE manager or team. Do we still need this module?
Not to build a new register, they likely already run one, and this module isn't asking you to duplicate it. What it adds is the connection: making sure their work is visibly linked to changes happening elsewhere on site, including anything built from the rest of this catalogue, since even a well-run HSE function can only act on a change it's actually told about. See guide section 4 and section 10.
What's the actual difference between HSE System and Safety Observation & Near-Miss System?
They sound like the same thing and aren't. HSE System is the compliance and systems layer: the statutory obligations a regulator, auditor, or insurer would ask to see evidence of, risk assessments, permits, equipment examination, incident reporting duties. Safety Observation & Near-Miss System, once built, is the daily behavioural layer: walking the floor, spotting hazards and near misses before they become incidents, the habit rather than the record.
Do I need special software, or can I just use a spreadsheet?
A spreadsheet is fine. The obligations register needs five things: obligation, regulation or driver, evidence held, review date, owner, status. However you hold that is your choice, spreadsheet, dedicated software, or an existing system that already does this. See guide section 15.
