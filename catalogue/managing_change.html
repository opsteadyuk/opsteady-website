Common questions

What if the person or group doesn't fit any of the four types?
Describe the resistance plainly in your own words, then pick a move suited to what you've actually described, rather than forcing a fit. The four types in section 5 are a starting point for naming a pattern, not a mandatory classification, and most sites find people who fit more than one or none cleanly. See section 14, fork 3.
What if I'm not the one who owns this change?
Build the same change map, but check your moves against the actual owner's plan before you act on them. A move that looks right in isolation can undercut the person who has to sponsor this upward. See section 5's minimum decision fork and section 14, fork 1.
Do I need Culture running already before I use this?
This module assumes Culture's response tracker exists in some working state, that's the baseline it picks up from. If it doesn't, this will still work on a single change, but treat "does my site raise problems openly" as a separate gap worth starting on, since this module depends on that existing rather than fixing it. See section 4 and section 14, fork 2.
What if nobody's positive about this yet?
That's a fair, honest finding this early, not a failure of the change map. Manufacturing a positive story before it's real is worse than having none, it reads as spin the moment someone checks it against the floor. Keep watching for it rather than assuming nobody will be. See section 5's positive-impact subsection, section 14, fork 5, and section 15.
How do I ask someone who's already doing well to take a bigger role, without it turning into a penalty for being good at their job?
Ask, don't assume, and check what it's actually costing them before you ask again. Someone genuinely ahead of the curve is easy to lean on precisely because they're coping, which can quietly turn into a penalty for competence rather than a real choice. An advocate who burns out does more damage to the change than one who was never asked. See section 15, the willing advocate.
