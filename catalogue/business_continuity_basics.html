Common questions

How is this different from HSE System?
HSE owns statutory safety and environmental compliance, obligations a site has to meet by law. This module owns operational resilience, what keeps the site running, or gets it running again, when something outside normal firefighting hits it, a supplier failure, a system outage, the site itself becoming unusable, a key person suddenly gone. Keep them as separate registers even on a site that has both. See guide section 1 and the boundary note at the top of the guide.
Do we need HSE System built first?
No — this module stands on its own regardless. The boundary between the two is conceptual, not a sequencing dependency. See guide section 9.
Won't this just duplicate an IT disaster-recovery plan?
Only if it's built as one. This module is deliberately operational, owned by whoever runs the site, not filed away as an IT document. Where a formal disaster-recovery function already exists, keep this register as the operational layer feeding into it rather than a duplicate. See guide section 13.
We've never had a major disruption. Do we really need this?
That's exactly the situation the guide names directly: a site that's never been tested often has the least honest picture of its own risk, not the safest one. The value of a plan only becomes obvious at the moment it's actually needed. See guide section 4 and section 16.
How many risks should actually get a full response plan?
The top handful, not everything on the initial list. List broadly, score honestly, then write full plans only for what's genuinely likely, high impact, or both. See guide section 4 and section 8.
