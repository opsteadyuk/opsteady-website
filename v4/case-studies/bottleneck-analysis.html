The station everyone blamed wasn't the one costing them the order.

Riverside Fabrication argued about Press 3 for six months. The real constraint was somewhere else entirely.

Home/ Bottleneck Analysis — case study

The assumption

Press 3 was the newest, least familiar machine — and the one blamed whenever a shipment ran late. Twice, the operations director queued a second operator for it. Twice, she pulled the request back, uneasy it kept being asked for without working.

What Opsteady established

A Health Check flagged Delivery & Planning as the weakest area — and named Bottleneck Analysis, not Capacity Planning, as the right first move. Run properly, it showed the real limit wasn't a press at all.

The decision

No second operator was approved. The response changed how the existing capacity was managed — not how much of it there was.

The result

180 → 224units/week, six weeks
£0spent on headcount

Riverside re-checks monthly — not because the constraint usually moves, but because fixing one tends to reveal the next.
