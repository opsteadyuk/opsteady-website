What it does

Names the real constraint, sizes the cost, and gets the free gains first

Picture a single-lane road with one slow car on it. That car sets the speed for every car behind it, no matter how fast they could go individually. Speed up any car except the slow one and nothing changes — the queue just moves somewhere else. A line works the same way: its output is set by its single slowest step, not by how busy or efficient every other step looks.

The tool confirms the constraint against two signals, not one measurement alone: where work-in-progress is genuinely piling up, and where the next step stands idle waiting. Then it walks you through exploit and subordinate — getting everything the constraint can already give you — before any capital request goes anywhere.

FAQ

Questions worth answering before you buy

How long do I actually need to log for?

A week is the minimum, not a guarantee. If your output genuinely varies by day of week or shift pattern, one week can point at the wrong step — log two weeks before naming anything.

Do I have to buy new equipment once I've found my constraint?

No — and buying too fast is one of the most common mistakes. Work through exploit and subordinate first: get everything the constraint can already give you, and pace every other station to it. Most sites find real, free gains here. Only a genuine, evidenced shortfall goes to Capacity Planning Model.

Why would I deliberately slow a station down that could run faster?

Because running it faster doesn't get you a single extra unit out the door — the constraint still sets the pace. It usually causes real harm instead: work piles up faster than the constraint can absorb it, or a shared resource gets pulled away from the constraint to keep the faster station fed.

What if the constraint turns out to be a person, not a machine?

The tool doesn't care what shape the constraint takes. If one person's pace sets the line's ceiling, that's a real finding, and it points toward cross-training or standard work, not blame.

What if my constraint moves during the week?

That's a real finding on multi-product or multi-shift sites, not a measurement error. Note both candidates, run one more full week, and let the pattern settle.
