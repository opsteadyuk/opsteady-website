Basic Problem Solving / 5 Why

Small problems get a real, checkable cause and a verified fix, at the level they happened — in minutes, not a project.

Overview

Without a shared, lightweight method, small problems tend to go one of two expensive ways: they get patched, the same defect fixed the same way for the third time this month with nobody asking why it keeps happening, or they get escalated by default into a full investigation because nobody trusts a lighter method to hold up. Under time pressure, the fastest available response is always the least effortful one — patch it and move on, or hand it upward and stop thinking about it.

This module teaches a lightweight, teachable version of the 5 Why method: describe the problem factually, ask why until the answer is both actionable and checkable — could someone specifically go and verify this is true — propose a countermeasure, and assign a named owner and a defined way to verify it actually held. Five is a guide, not a rule; the chain stops when the cause passes the checkable test, whether that's the third why or the seventh. It is deliberately not a competing method to Root Cause Analysis (P7.5) or A3 (P7.4) — it's the everyday tool that sits below them, and feeds them cleanly when a problem genuinely needs a fuller investigation.

Done properly, supervisors and team leaders use the method on appropriate problems without being prompted, countermeasures are verified rather than just written down, and completed sheets get reviewed lightly in an existing rhythm — a tier meeting or coaching conversation — checking specifically for a "why" chain that stopped at a convenient answer, usually a person, rather than a genuine mechanism.

At a glance
Time

Most 5 Whys take 5 to 15 minutes to run once the people involved are standing at the problem. Building the one-page form and teaching the method takes under an hour.

Who's involved

The person who found or owns the problem, and anyone who can speak directly to what actually happened, not a manager reconstructing events secondhand.

For your own site, decide: who's expected to use the method day to day, usually every supervisor and team leader; who checks whether a "why" answer is genuinely checkable versus a guess dressed up as a cause, a coaching role reviewing sheets in an existing rhythm; who owns each countermeasure once it's agreed, named on the form and carried onto the action register; and who decides when a problem is too big for this method and needs to escalate, agreed in advance so it isn't argued fresh every time.

What you'll need

A one-page 5 Why or basic problem-solving sheet. No specialist software or training beyond the method itself. Confirm a basic action-tracking place, the Action & Countermeasure Tracking System (P2.7), already exists so a countermeasure doesn't simply disappear once it's written down. The working file, P7.8_Basic_Problem_Solving_5Why_Sheet.xlsx, two tabs: a Problem sheet (carrying seven why columns, not five, since five is a guide not a rule, and refusing to treat a row as finished until the cause is confirmed both actionable and checkable — it also asks what kind of cause you landed on, flagging where the answer is a person or their attention) and an Action register link tab that reads each finished row and states plainly whether it's ready to commit, still unfinished, or belongs in Root Cause Analysis or A3 instead.

Before you start

Agree upfront, explicitly, that not every problem needs a full Root Cause Analysis or A3, and that this lighter method is a legitimate, complete response to the great majority of small, local problems, not a lesser substitute waiting to be escalated anyway.

Work out first whether the problem, as described, looks small and local, or already shows signs of being bigger, recurring, or crossing more than one team or function: a small, local problem runs the standard sequence; a problem that's already recurred despite a previous fix, or clearly involves more than one team's process, is a signal to escalate to Root Cause Analysis or A3 rather than force it through this lighter method.
