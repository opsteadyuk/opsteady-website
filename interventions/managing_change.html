Managing Change That Sticks

Plan for what makes changes fail, not just what makes them launch.

£179Buy now
Overview

A well-sequenced, well-resourced change launches fine and is quietly gone eight months later, because nobody planned for the part after the launch enthusiasm fades. Change plans are built around the launch — the training, the new board, the kick-off meeting — which answers "how do we start this" but never "who's going to quietly let this slide once nobody's watching, and what do we do about that in advance." The launch is the easy ten percent.

This module builds a floor-level map around one real, named change in motion, not a hypothetical: who's affected in each of three directions — down, across, up — what type of resistance they show, and the specific move suited to that pattern, not a generic communications plan. It covers the gap most change plans miss entirely too: every change also has people already quietly for it, and that energy is usually wasted because a plan built entirely around managing resistance never asks who's already on side or what to do with them — a used advocate moves a change faster than a managed sceptic ever will. It assumes Culture (F3) is already in some working state and picks up a narrower question: for this specific change, who resists it, why, and what you actually do about each one.

Done properly, every affected group is named with a move assigned, including at least one upward move, the moves actually get made within the first week rather than just written down, and the map gets reopened when something doesn't go as expected — the real test is whether the change is still visibly running at six, eight, and twelve months out.

At a glance
Time

About an hour for the first map, on one real change already underway.

Who's involved

You, and ideally the change's actual owner if that isn't you.

For your own site, decide: who actually owns this change, you or someone you're supporting; who has the authority to adjust the plan if the map surfaces a problem; who else needs to see the finished map, and who it should stay private to; and who's already positive about this, and whether you've actually planned to use that or just been glad it's there.

What you'll need

The change map template, and Culture's response tracker if you already have one running — this module assumes that baseline exists, not zero; if Culture isn't in any use yet, start there. The working file, E2_Change_Map_Template.docx, a single printable page: who's affected, direction (down, across, up), type (resistance or positive, which of five patterns it fits, or a plain description), the specific move including how a positive-impact person gets used, and a check-back date.

Before you start

Don't use this on a change that's still hypothetical or hasn't been announced — it works on something real and in motion, where you can name actual people and actual reactions rather than guessing at a category of resistance.

Work out first whether you own this change or are supporting someone else through it: if you own it, build the map and decide the moves directly; if you're supporting it, build the same map but check your moves against the actual owner's before acting, since a move that looks right to you might undercut the person who has to sponsor this upward.
