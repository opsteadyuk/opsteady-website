Shift Handover System

Five things stated every time, no exceptions, so the day's truth doesn't get rewritten shift by shift and nothing outstanding falls through the gap.

Overview

The moment the day's truth changes hands between shifts is usually the least structured moment in the whole daily rhythm. One shift knows what actually happened; the next shift inherits whatever got mentioned in passing on the way out the door. In a reactive week that gap compounds — the incoming shift acts on yesterday's picture while today is already writing a new one.

The fix is one rule: five fixed fields — safety, quality, production status, outstanding actions, anything abnormal — stated at every handover, every time, even when the honest answer is "no issues," because a blank field is indistinguishable from a forgotten check. Outstanding actions get special treatment: every item passed between shifts needs a name and a date on the Actions Tracker, not just a mention in the log, since a mentioned problem is everyone's responsibility, which in practice means nobody's. The same underlying reason checklists work in aviation and surgery applies here: memory under time pressure is unreliable in a specific, predictable way — people remember what was dramatic and forget what was routine but necessary, and a structured field forces the routine check precisely when nothing dramatic happened, which is most of the time.

Done well, completeness stays high across all five fields, and — the real test — an outsider covering a shift can read the last handover and know what's going on without asking anyone.

At a glance
Time

Half an hour to agree the five things as a group, then a couple of minutes per handover from then on — under 2 minutes done honestly is the design target that keeps it running under pressure instead of being the first thing skipped.

Who's involved

Whoever actually hands over — team leaders or shift supervisors — in the room together when the five things get agreed. Everyone doing a handover needs to know before the first one runs under the new system, not after.

What you'll need

The workbook, printed or on a shared device at the handover point. The working file itself has four tabs: Definitions (the five things, written once, plus who owns the standard and when it was set), Handover Log, Actions Tracker (raised date, action, owner, due date, status, closed date, days open — calculated automatically), and a Dashboard that calculates completeness, open actions, and average age of open actions from the other two tabs.

Before you start

Agree your five things in writing, with the people who'll actually be using them, and agree who owns the standard — the default five fields work for most sites as written, but if yours genuinely needs different headings, change them once, in writing, on the Definitions tab, not shift by shift. Expect it to feel slower than the old way for about three days — that's normal, not a sign it isn't working.
