8 Wastes (TIMWOODS) Identification Toolkit

A shared language for seeing what's actually costing time and money — everyone on the floor able to name the eight wastes and spot the ones that genuinely hurt this site, not just recite the acronym.

Overview

Most sites without this module jump straight from a problem to a proposed solution, without ever agreeing on what, specifically, is wasteful about the current state. Different people looking at the same process often see completely different things — one sees a quality problem, another sees people standing around, a third sees stock piling up — and without a shared vocabulary, these read as unrelated complaints rather than what they usually are: several named wastes sitting inside the same process. Waste is often invisible specifically because it's familiar — a long walk to a distant parts store, a batch size set "to be safe" years ago, stops registering as a problem simply because it's how things have always been done here.

This module teaches the eight classic wastes of lean, TIMWOODS — Transport, Inventory, Motion, Waiting, Over-production, Over-processing, Defects, and Skills (under-utilised talent) — and a simple method for spotting real examples of each in the site's own processes, not from a textbook. It is deliberately lighter than Value Stream Mapping and calculates no cost: its entire job is a shared way to see and name waste, from which a ranked list of what actually matters most emerges, with the vocabulary and the mapping working best in sequence, this module first.

Done well, the eight wastes turn up unprompted in ordinary conversation — a handover, a tier meeting — and at least one top-ranked waste has a live, tracked action against it, not just a good list produced once and never revisited.

At a glance
Time

No special data required. Half a day is enough for a genuine first pass across one area or product family.

Who's involved

One or two people who do the work every day, since they'll spot real examples a manager walking through alone would miss entirely. A facilitator to keep the walk moving through all eight wastes rather than stalling on the first one or two that are easiest to spot.

For your own site, decide: who chooses the area to walk first, usually whoever owns the improvement priority for that part of the site; who facilitates the walk itself; who owns turning the ranked list into an actual action, named per top-ranked waste; and who decides when to re-walk the same area, tied to a real trigger rather than left to memory.

What you'll need

A notebook or simple waste-spotting sheet, one page per waste or a single summary board. No specialist equipment or software.

Before you start

Choose one area or product family to walk, not the whole site at once, and confirm the people joining are genuinely available for the full half-day — a rushed, partial walk tends to produce shallow, generic examples rather than real ones. Agree upfront that all eight wastes get a genuine attempt, not just the two or three that are easiest to spot from a casual glance.

Give Over-processing and Skills waste deliberate extra effort — they're consistently the hardest two to spot on a casual walk, since they require understanding why a step exists or what a person is genuinely capable of, and they're the two most often skipped or filled in with a token example. Prompt for them directly: which steps here duplicate a check done elsewhere, and is anyone on this walk doing work well below their actual skill.
