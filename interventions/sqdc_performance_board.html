Overview

Most sites already have performance data — it just lives scattered across a shift log, a quality system, a scheduling tool and a spreadsheet somebody updates on Fridays, and none of those four places talk to each other. A problem that shows up in the quality system on Tuesday doesn't reach anyone who can act on it until the numbers are pulled together, usually a week later, usually after it's happened three more times.

This module applies Visual Management Foundations' (F4) standard/actual/gap-and-response method to one fixed set of four categories — safety, quality, delivery, cost — so the same four things that matter on every site get checked every shift, in one place, by the same routine. Each metric pulls its exact definition from the site's KPI Definitions register (F1.2) rather than being invented for the board itself, and it's built at the point of use, not in a manager's office, because a board nobody walks past has already failed the test this module is built around.

The tell for whether it's genuinely working is the gap-and-response cell: filled in with something specific and checkable, or left blank — a board that's green across all four categories every day for weeks is measuring the wrong things, not a site with no problems. Done properly, it becomes the object the site's first-tier meeting actually reviews, turning a miss into same-shift action instead of a discovery a week later.

At a glance
Time

Half a day to build the board and agree the first metric per category, once KPI definitions already exist.

Who's involved

Whoever owns each category's number day to day. Whoever's agreed the site's KPI definitions (F1.2), since the board reads from that register rather than inventing its own version of a metric. Whoever chairs the first-tier meeting the board feeds into.

For your own site, decide: who updates each category every shift, and what happens if they're away; who fills in the gap-and-response cell — the same person updating the number, or whoever owns fixing it; and who reviews the board daily, and whether that's the same person chairing the first-tier meeting.

What you'll need

Agreed KPI definitions for at least four measures (F1.2) — a board metric with no matching register entry is a number someone invented for the board, and it won't survive the first time someone questions it. A location for the board at the point of use, not in an office. A decision on physical or digital.

Before you start

Check whether something already calling itself a performance board exists on site, even one that's gone quiet — if so, audit it against the fixed spine (every category shows standard, actual and gap-and-response; a named updater; reviewed daily at the first-tier meeting) rather than starting from nothing. Most boards that have gone quiet are missing the gap-and-response column entirely, or have it but nobody's filled it in for weeks. Tell everyone whose shift feeds a number onto the board before the first update, and tell whoever runs the first-tier meeting, since the board becomes the object that meeting reviews. Don't launch all four categories at once — get one category genuinely live and updated every shift without chasing before adding the second.
