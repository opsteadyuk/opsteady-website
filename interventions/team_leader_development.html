Team Leader Development Pathway

A real path from operator to team leader, built on observable capability, not a job title handed to whoever happened to be next.

£179Buy now
Overview

Team leaders are often promoted for being a good operator, then given a genuinely different job with no structured preparation behind it, because sites still promote on gut feel — whoever's been there longest, or whoever a manager happens to trust — with nothing written down and nothing a candidate could point to. An operator's job is defined and repeatable; a team leader's job is structurally different — checking, coaching, reacting to things that can't be fully defined in advance — so being excellent at the first job says very little about readiness for the second.

This pathway defines four stages, from ready to develop through to fully capable, and at each stage names the observable capabilities a candidate needs, drawn from the same four categories — Safety, Quality, People, Delivery — a team leader's own round will eventually cover, with a real development activity attached to each one and a named person signing off progression against evidence, not time served. It depends on three other modules already being in at least partial use — Skills Matrix (P1.1), Training Plan & Competency Sign-off (P1.2), and Supervisor Standard Work Pack (P2.4) — since without them there's nothing real to develop toward, though it can be built alongside them rather than strictly after.

Done properly, at least one candidate has a live tracker with a named next step, evidence behind every signed-off capability rather than time served, and team leaders arriving in the role have already demonstrated the job's real capabilities rather than just having proven themselves a good operator.

At a glance
Time

Half a day to define your stages and capabilities the first time. Ongoing, a short review per candidate, monthly or against whatever rhythm your site already runs, reviewed quarterly overall or against your site's existing performance-conversation cadence.

Who's involved

Whoever currently makes promotion decisions, plus the candidate's own supervisor, who's best placed to observe the capabilities in practice — rarely the same person for all four categories. For your own site: who currently makes promotion decisions, and are they willing to sign off against evidence rather than instinct; which one or two people are already informally "next," whether or not that's been said out loud; and who's best placed to observe each capability.

What you'll need

Skills Matrix (P1.1), Training Plan & Competency Sign-off (P1.2), and Supervisor Standard Work Pack (P2.4) in at least partial use — not fully built, just real enough to develop toward. The working file ships as P1.4_Team_Leader_Development_Pathway_Workbook.xlsx, four tabs: How to use, Pathway map, Individual progress tracker, and a removable Example.

Before you start

Check honestly whether the three upstream modules are in at least partial use — if not, build this pathway alongside them, not before them. Tell anyone informally seen as "next" plainly what the pathway is, and that it replaces gut feel with something they can actually see and work toward.
