Understanding the P&L

Reading your own site's numbers well enough to know what a shopfloor decision is actually worth, before you have to defend it.

£179Buy now
Overview

Most operational decisions on a manufacturing site are made and defended in operational language — minutes, units, headcount, percentage uptime. The board and finance judge the site in a different language — margin, cost per unit, payback. When nobody translates between the two, good decisions go uncredited, and teams avoid engaging finance at all because the language feels foreign rather than because the case is weak.

This isn't a finance course. It's a plain-language walk through the site's own P&L, connecting numbers already tracked — waste, overtime, downtime, labour — to the lines finance and the board actually read, then running one real shopfloor decision through to its actual P&L effect, start to finish, so the mechanic lands as something done, not something read about. The worked example shows why this matters beyond vocabulary: a 1% shift in direct labour is worth roughly six times the same shift in waste on a representative site P&L, which is exactly the kind of thing that should decide where limited attention goes.

Done properly, someone on the team can explain, in their own words, what their decision costs or earns, without needing a translator standing next to them afterward — and cases built with this literacy get credited and resourced more often than ones told purely in operational language.

At a glance
Time

2 to 3 hours to walk a real P&L line by line the first time. Considerably less once it's familiar.

Who's involved

Whoever leads the site, plus a finance contact who can supply or sanity-check a representative P&L — finance isn't required to run the session, only to have supplied numbers close enough to real to be worth trusting.

For your own site, decide: whose P&L you're actually using — the whole site, one line, one product family; who can supply real or representative numbers, and how current they need to be; and which shopfloor decision you'll run through the walkthrough first.

What you'll need

A real or representative P&L for the site. A simplified one from finance is enough — exact audited figures aren't required for this to be useful. The working file, P8.3_PL_Walkthrough_and_Worked_Examples.xlsx, two tabs: a P&L walkthrough sheet (each line of a standard site P&L with a plain-language explanation, whether an Opsteady tracker already measures it, and your own figure for the period, calculating each line's share of revenue) and a Worked example template (one row per decision traced through — its operational size, what it's worth, the P&L line it touches, and the tracker that produced the number).

Before you start

Get a copy of the site's real or representative P&L first — if one genuinely isn't accessible yet, run the illustrative example in section 2 to land the mechanic, then revisit with real numbers once they're available. Tell finance, if their numbers are being used, that this is for operational literacy, not a request to audit or challenge their figures.

If the site runs multiple product lines or carries shared overhead, a single simplified P&L won't fit cleanly — that allocation logic is a genuine finance judgement call, not something to generalise safely, so involve finance directly before treating one line's figures as the whole picture.
