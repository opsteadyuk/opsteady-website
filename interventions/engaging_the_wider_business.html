Engaging the Wider Business

HR, finance and sales showing up as active participants in shopfloor improvement, not spectators who hear about it afterward, or approvers who can quietly block it.

£179Buy now
Overview

Improvement work on most sites stays siloed on the shop floor until it either breaks — because it clashed with something outside operations nobody thought to check — or fails to get resourced, because nobody built the case in language finance or the board actually trusts.

This module is a stake-check, not a standing committee: for a live improvement initiative, identify who else genuinely has a stake — HR for labour and contract implications, finance for cost and payback framing, sales for customer-commitment implications — define specifically what you want from them, a real question they can actually answer, not a status update, and invite them in early enough that their input can still change the outcome. It's deliberately distinct from Managing Change That Sticks (E2): that module navigates resistance to one specific, named change already underway; this one is standing cross-functional participation in improvement work generally, whether or not any particular change is in motion.

Done properly, at least one improvement carries direct input from outside operations that genuinely changed something — the framing, the scope or the plan itself — not input asked for and then quietly ignored, which is named as one of the fastest ways this teaches an invited function their input doesn't matter.

At a glance
Time

An hour to identify who has a genuine stake in a given initiative and define the specific ask, before you invite anyone in.

Who's involved

Whoever owns the improvement initiative itself, and a named contact in each function with a real stake — HR, finance, sales — not "whoever's free." For your own site, decide: which live initiative is the strongest candidate, one where outside input would genuinely change the outcome; which function has the clearest real stake, and who's the named contact there; and what specifically you want from them, stated as a real question, not a general update.

What you'll need

One live improvement initiative that could genuinely use outside input, real, not hypothetical. The working file, E8_Stakeholder_Checklist_and_Engagement_Log.xlsx, two tabs: Who else has a stake (one row per kind of initiative, refusing to treat a row as ready until the specific question to ask is written, and counting how many times each row has actually been used) and an Engagement log (one row per initiative actually run through it — the function invited, the specific ask, when they were invited, what came back, and what changed as a result, recording an invitation issued after the decision was made as exactly that).

Before you start

Match the stake to the right function rather than asking generally: labour or contract implications point to HR, a cost claim or capital ask points to finance, a change to sequencing, batch size or lead time points to sales — and if none genuinely apply, proceed without engaging anyone else, since manufacturing a stake where none exists wastes the credibility this habit depends on. Tell the function being invited exactly what's being asked of them before the conversation starts, a specific question, not an open invitation to a meeting with no agenda.
