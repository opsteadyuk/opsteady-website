Overview

Most sites already have a number for on-time delivery, and a number for right first time. Very few can say, without checking, exactly what those numbers count and what they leave out. Two people, or the same person at two different points, independently decide what "on time" or "right" means without ever agreeing it on purpose — one counts from the date the customer asked for, another from the date the site promised; one RFT figure quietly excludes documentation, another doesn't. Nobody's lying, nobody's even wrong exactly, they're answering a question that was never asked out loud, until two people are arguing about a figure that means different things to each of them.

OTIF and RFT sit together on one dashboard because they answer two different questions about the same customer promise — OTIF asks whether the thing arrived when and as promised, RFT asks whether it was right the first time — and a site can be strong on one and weak on the other without either number showing it, because they're rarely read side by side. This tool starts by making you write both definitions down on the Definitions tab before a single order is logged, then logs every completed order and finished batch against them, with a real cause recorded every time something misses, not guessed at once a quarter.

Done well, the top Pareto cause on either log becomes the next improvement project, not a line read out in a meeting and forgotten — and because everything on the Dashboard is calculated from the two logs, a number that suddenly jumps is a prompt to check the Definitions tab before celebrating, not a result to take at face value.

At a glance
Time

Half a day to set your definitions and get the log started, then five minutes a day logging as orders close.

Who's involved

Whoever currently owns the OTIF or RFT number, if one already exists — this isn't a solo desk exercise, the definition needs to survive contact with the people who'll argue about it. For your own site, decide: who decides the definition stays as it currently sits, once set; who's allowed to propose a change to it; who gets told when it changes, even if they don't need to agree; and, if this number already gets reported upward, who needs to agree first before a change goes live.

What you'll need

The workbook, and access to whatever you currently use to see promise dates and delivered quantities — an ERP export, a delivery note pile, a whiteboard. You're logging from that system, not replacing it. The working file, P4.5_OTIF_RFT_Workbook.xlsx, four tabs: Definitions, OTIF Log, RFT Log, and a Dashboard that calculates everything from the two logs, nothing typed by hand except the review period dates.

Before you start

Define your own OTIF and RFT before logging a single order — there's no industry-standard formula for either, and the only wrong answer is not writing one down. Work out first whether you're building from nothing (answer the Definitions tab questions in order) or auditing an existing number (run the same questions as a check on what it's silently assuming, particularly which date "on time" actually measures from). If OTIF or RFT already gets reported upward, tell whoever receives that report before you touch the definition.
