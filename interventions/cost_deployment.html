Cost Deployment

Improvement effort and management attention follow the money, not whichever loss shouted loudest this week.

£179Buy now
Overview

Most sites without this module set improvement priorities by noise, habit or local pain — whichever problem is most visible or most recently painful, rather than what actually costs the most. A machine that broke down yesterday gets attention; a slow, chronic quality issue that's cost more over the year gets none, because nobody has ever put the two on the same scale. Operations and finance often end up talking past each other entirely, one describing losses in units and downtime minutes, the other in numbers on a P&L, with no shared translation between the two.

This module takes the losses a site already knows about — downtime, scrap, overtime, quality escapes — and turns them into a single ranked view: what each major loss category costs the site per year, roughly, its trend, who owns it, and which method or module is the primary way to attack it. It's not a new way of measuring loss, and not a full Activity-Based Costing system, but a way of putting existing measurements into one shared currency, built from data the site already has and kept simple enough that the first few losses can be ranked in a week, not a quarter. Every estimate names its source, and no matrix gets published until someone genuinely finance-literate has challenged the numbers against the real statement — a caveated, roughly-right estimate ranked against other losses is more useful than no estimate at all, and what matters is whether the ranking order would survive a more careful recalculation, not pinpoint precision on any single figure.

Done properly, the top-ranked losses each have a named owner, the ranking is genuinely used out loud when deciding what to work on next — "that's not in our top five, it can wait" — and at least one high-cost loss shows a measurable reduction traceable to a specific action taken because of where it sat on the list.

At a glance
Time

A few hours to build the first-pass matrix once the underlying loss data already exists — most of the effort is pulling together numbers that already live in separate places, not generating new data.

Who's involved

Whoever owns the operational side of prioritisation, usually a plant or operations manager, plus access to someone finance-literate who can sanity-check the cost estimates against the real P&L.

For your own site, decide: who builds the first-pass matrix, usually the operations lead with visibility across the major loss categories; who challenges the cost estimates, a named finance or P&L-aware person whose job is to sanity-check, not rubber-stamp; who owns each ranked loss category individually, so accountability isn't shared or implied; and who decides the refresh rhythm and who's authorised to challenge the ranking if a number looks wrong.

What you'll need

Existing loss data: Waste & Cost-of-Quality Calculator's (P8.1) output, OEE loss trees, downtime logs, scrap records, overtime tracking, and customer complaint costs where available — no specialist costing software is required for the standard version. The working file, P8.4_Cost_Deployment_Matrix.xlsx, one tab deliberately: one row per loss category holding the estimated annual cost, where the estimate came from, current trend, named owner, primary improvement method, and whether it's been sanity-checked, ranked and shared-of-total calculated automatically, plus four boxes tracking refresh rhythm, last-refreshed date, who may challenge a number, and where it's published — the sheet counts down to the next refresh and marks itself OVERDUE once that passes.

Before you start

Use existing data as the starting point rather than launching a fresh measurement exercise — this module organises and ranks what the site already measures, it doesn't ask the site to start measuring from nothing. Accept that early estimates will be rough, and treat "roughly right and ranked" as more useful than "precisely calculated for one category and missing for the rest."

Work out first whether reliable cost-of-loss data already exists for most of the site's major loss categories: if it does, build the matrix directly from what's already tracked; if it doesn't for some categories, build a rough, clearly caveated first-pass estimate rather than waiting for perfect data, and refine it on the next refresh.
