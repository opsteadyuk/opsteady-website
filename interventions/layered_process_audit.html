Layered Process Audit System

Process drift caught by more than one level, on a schedule, before it turns into a defect nobody saw coming.

£349Buy now
Overview

Final inspection catches a defect after it already exists — it can't catch the upstream process step that actually caused it, and by the time a bad batch reaches inspection the material's used and the only question left is ship or scrap. Standards can exist on paper, from Set Standards or a written work instruction, with nobody confirming day to day whether they're actually followed, so the only real signal something's drifting ends up being a customer complaint or a scrapped batch.

This module fixes it by splitting checking across three genuinely different people on three different rhythms against the same set of checkpoints: the team leader or operator checking daily, the supervisor or quality lead sampling weekly, the plant or ops manager confirming monthly — not just that the process is holding, but that the daily and weekly layers actually ran. Every checkpoint scoring below the top band gets a named action with an owner and a date, not a number filed and forgotten. One layer alone can't see everything: a team leader sees one shift, a supervisor sampling across shifts can see a pattern no single shift reveals, and a manager confirming both layers ran sees whether the whole system is holding.

Done properly, all three layers are running on schedule against written checkpoints, every below-standard score has a named action, and a manager can say honestly that both layers beneath them are actually running, not just that the line is.

At a glance
Time

Half a day to agree checkpoints and the rotation. After that, daily checks take minutes, the weekly sample 20 to 30 minutes, the monthly confirmation about an hour.

Who's involved

A named person at each of the three layers — team leader or operator, supervisor or quality lead, plant or ops manager — and they have to be genuinely different people, not one person wearing three hats, or the structure loses the exact visibility it exists to provide. For your own site, decide: which process steps genuinely matter most if they drift; who's the named person at each layer, and are they genuinely different people; and who owns turning a low score into an action with a date.

What you'll need

The process standards this audits against should already exist, from Set Standards (F2.1), before a single checkpoint is defined — you cannot meaningfully score compliance to a standard that was never written down. The working file: P2.6_Layered_Process_Audit_System_Workbook.xlsx, five tabs — How to use, Audit schedule, Scoring sheet (with automatic score-summary and layer-coverage blocks), Findings and actions, and a removable worked Example.

Before you start

Pick one area, one process, before expanding — the same pilot-first discipline as any scored audit. Tell everyone whose process gets checked before the first check happens, framed as catching drift early, not catching people out. Confirm honestly whether this is a first audit on the process (score for an accurate baseline) or a repeat (score the same way, then check specifically whether last cycle's low-scoring checkpoints got their action closed).
