Safety Observation & Near-Miss System

Near misses and unsafe conditions caught and logged before one of them becomes an injury.

£179Buy now
Overview

A near miss usually gets mentioned in passing — someone notices a loose guard, a trailing cable, a near collision between a forklift and a pedestrian, says something to whoever's nearby and moves on. Nothing gets written down, so nothing gets fixed, and the same close call keeps happening quietly until eventually it isn't a near miss anymore.

This module logs every near miss and unsafe condition the moment it's seen, categorised briefly and closed out with a visible action, trended by area over time so risk shows up where it's genuinely concentrated, not just where the last injury happened to occur. It carries a stated precondition, not a soft link: this module doesn't create psychological safety, it depends on it — Culture (F3.1) is what makes reporting possible at all, since the moment someone believes raising something gets them blamed rather than heard, reporting dries up regardless of how good the reporting method is. It sits next to, not inside, HSE System (E9.1): that owns the formal compliance record, this owns the daily behavioural layer, with the escalation threshold between them a site-set decision.

Done properly, reports come in regularly and the count rises over time, not falls — a healthy site gets more reports, not fewer, because people are actually looking and actually believe it's worth their time, and the injury trend, not the report count, is the real signal of whether things are improving.

At a glance
Time

An hour to agree the reporting method and where the log lives. After that, near-zero setup cost — the whole point is removing friction, not adding a project.

Who's involved

Whoever's closest to the work — operators and team leaders who see conditions day to day. Someone who owns closing the loop, since the log's credibility depends entirely on that person actually doing it. For your own site, decide: who receives reports and logs them the same day, without exception; who owns closing each action out, and by when; and who reviews the trend monthly, and what the threshold is for escalating something into HSE's formal register.

What you'll need

A card, a laminated tag, a simple form, or just telling a supervisor who logs it the same day — whichever is genuinely fastest for someone to use in the moment. A log, spreadsheet or paper, and a place to post the trend where the reporting team can actually see it. The working file, E9.2_Safety_Observation_Near_Miss_System_Workbook.xlsx, three tabs: an Observation log (date, area, what was observed, what could have happened, category, reported by, action taken, owner, closed date, days to close calculated), a Trend by area tab, and a Trend by category tab against a fixed five-item dropdown — machine guarding, housekeeping, near-collision, manual handling, other.

Before you start

Work out first whether the barrier to reporting on your site is friction or trust: friction means people would report if it were easier, so fix the reporting method; trust means people don't believe reporting is safe, and no reporting method fixes that on its own — address the Culture dependency directly first, or this module sits unused however well it's designed. Tell whoever runs HSE System, since the escalation threshold from this log into HSE's formal register needs agreeing between the two, and whoever leads Culture work on site, since this module's success is a direct read on whether that work has genuinely taken hold. Start in one area first, not a site-wide rollout, and prove the loop closes properly in one place before people are asked to trust it elsewhere.
